## 1. Numbers vs captions — the defects that break trust

1. **B1's simulated season has no spells, and the whole page says it does.** The stand-in strip is generated from the key's `fit_long.P_true` = [[0.7, 0.25, 0.05], [0.3, 0.5, 0.2], [0.1, 0.5, 0.4]] — a matrix chosen to test the fitter, not to look like a monsoon. Its mean run is **2.4 days**; the memoryless coin with the same colour mix runs **1.7 days**. The standfirst ("rain comes in spells"), F0's hotspot ① ("Spells are long here — rain comes and stays away in stretches, not in two-day flickers"), the prose after F0 ("Spells are long, not two days") and F4's observe ("The machine's strip has the season's spells: mean run 2.4 days against 2.4; the coin's 1.7 flickers") all describe setosa's 0.9-sticky weather while the screen shows a strip that flickers. The storyboard's nine-day dry run is a six-day one (the ten-day run crosses a row break and the hotspot cannot box it). The labelling of "simulated" is exemplary — a flag on the artefact, bold in the prompt, repeated in F1 and F4 — but a labelled stand-in still has to show the thing the text points at. Until the Bengaluru file lands, generate the season from a sticky matrix (diagonal ≈ 0.85 / 0.6 / 0.5, mean spell ≥ 5 days) so the lesson is on screen; F4's "which is the season?" is otherwise a chain being told apart from a chain.
2. **A5 F4 contradicts itself at its default setting.** The readouts print **home 1.02 %** and **forgets half in 31.8 days**; the observe underneath says "The computer's own drip is 0.023 %² a day with keep 0.875, **home 0.97 %, half-life 27.9 days**." Both are the engine's — the figure fixes β by variance targeting at the ten-year average cup (so the home is 1.02 % whatever the drip and only the half-life moves), while the observe quotes the key's free fit — but a fresher sees two homes and two half-lives for "the computer's drip" in one frame. The same observe ends "Push cup + keep past 1 and the drip is never leaked away: the bucket overflows" — with β = 1 − α − ω/V̄ the dial can never do that. And the word "hole" means three things across F2/F4: the leaked fraction (F2), "10 % of each cup is kept" (F4 prompt), "hole leaks 12 % a day" (the bucket drawing, 1 − β).
3. **A5's collapsed close quotes Hull numbers that are not Hull's yen fit.** "Hull's yen example gives the textbook numbers (ω = 0.0000013, α = 0.083, β = 0.914, long-run 1.4 % a day) and lives in the tests." 4. **A4 F4's picture cannot show what its number says.** The Dickey–Fuller plot runs ±60 on x and ±15 on y; the regression slope is −0.022, so the fitted line drops 1.3 units across the whole frame and lies on top of the dashed "slope 0: a drunk" line (the two labels overlap too). ## 10. Verdict

These five do what the brief asked and each has a frame I would show a desk head: A4's ten unlabelled strips with the test's own misses printed under them; A5's two identical tiles under a strip with storms and a strip without, and the squares divided by the band collapsing into fuzz; B1's "the day after each heavy day" row and the bus stop reading 9.8 minutes; B2's meter turning red at +10.11 under a fan lifted whole; B3's three drawn gauges, one pinned, one spinning, one landing on "the clock". The real NIFTY strip now runs through three chapters as one object, which is worth more than any single figure once the pages say so.

What a sharp reader would stop at is, again, numbers beside pictures that disagree with them: B1's season has no spells while every sentence on the page says it does; A5 F4 prints two homes and two half-lives for one drip and describes a move the dial cannot make; A4 F4's drag puts the rubber band on top of the drunk; A4 F2's random walk is a counter; A5's collapsed close quotes a yen fit that is not the yen fit; and the phrase "the answer key" appears nine times on pages meant for a tired fresher. None of it is a redesign — items 1–6 are a day, 7–9 another — and the B1 fix is a matrix. Fix 1–6 and I would put A5, B2 and B3 in front of the firm as they stand; A4 after item 3; B1 when it has either a sticky stand-in or the real rain.
