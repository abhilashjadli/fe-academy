_Read: charter §2 (incl. lightness amendment) and §3; `research/r1/A3.md`; `research/storyboards/A3.md`; `Chapter.tsx` and all eight figure files; the clip script; 14 screenshots (light/dark, 1100 and 390 px). ## 1. Coverage against the syllabus and the note

**What is taught, and taught well.** The chapter's one sentence — "A fitted line is only half the answer … every 'how sure' number under it is a lie" — is honoured from standfirst to conclusion, and the four failures of the syllabus line (heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation, multicollinearity, plus spurious regression as the note's fourth) are all present with a fix each: White, Newey–West / difference, drop-or-combine / VIF, difference / "R² > DW". Robust SEs are not just named but shown as three t-ratios on one slope (F3 step 6) — the Tsay p. 87 move, reproduced. Durbin–Watson is given as a habit, not a formula. The levels-vs-changes hedge ratio has stakes. This is more than Seeing Theory, setosa or any Statistics By Jim page attempts in one place.

**"Why OLS" is present but thin.** F1 says "the line is the one that makes the shaded squares as small as possible in total — that is all 'least squares' means", and F6 lets you drag points against the squares. That is the setosa move and it is enough for a fresher. What is *missing* is why squares rather than absolute gaps, and the one-liner that the leftovers sum to zero and are uncorrelated with x (note §6 "the projection fact that Panel 1 draws") — the note promised it in Panel 1; Panel 1 does not draw it.

**R² is never explained.** It is used in F0 before anything else ("read the score, R²"), as if a fresher knew it. The charter says assume nothing beyond intro probability. The StatQuest structure (SS around the mean vs SS around the fit; R² = the fraction of wobble the line removed) is one sentence and one picture; the chapter has neither. A reader who does not know R² cannot place a bet in F0, so the opening bet is partially blind.

**t-stats and p-values are used, not taught.** "|t|" appears in F0, F4 ("pass the textbook test (|t| > 2)"), F3 ("t = slope / SE"). "Textbook test" and "2" are never explained; the ± 2·SE convention is never tied to the normal from 0.2. One collapsed panel — "SE is the spread of the slope you'd get from a fresh sample; ±2 SE is the 95 % band from chapter 0.2; t = slope/SE; |t| > 2 is 'the band excludes zero'" — would close the gap for the whole chapter.

**Endogeneity**: absent, and the note's §6 says it should be "one panel … deferred to G1". Not even the one sentence. **In-sample vs out-of-sample**: absent. **Outliers / leverage / non-linearity** (the Anscombe III, IV, II failures): absent — and these are the *first* failures a fresher meets, before any time-series failure. A chapter titled "regression and its failure modes" that has no outlier and no curve is over-weighted toward the time-series failures (three of four "liars" are about time order).

**Over-weighted**: spurious regression gets F0, F2 panel 4, F4 (three rounds), and F5's "wander" — roughly half the page. Heteroskedasticity gets one comic panel whose numbers do not land (below) and a toggle in F5 where the fan is invisible (below).

**Internal contradiction a fresher will trip on.** The standfirst: "The slope is usually fine." F3 step 5: "the slope itself moves, because a hedge on levels is a hedge on the trend" (0.77 → 1.37, nearly double). F3 step 6, one click later: "The slope never changed; only how loudly you may say 'sure'." The conclusion: "none of them changes the line much." For the Drunks and for a drifting beta the slope is *not* fine — it is the wrong number — and the chapter says so and unsays it on consecutive steps.

## 2. Figure by figure

**F0 — two coin piles (game + bet).** Mode is right (the note's strongest hook). Two problems. (a) The storyboard promised "Pure noise gives R² = 0.8 within a few tries"; the chapter's own measured rate (`rateOver`) prints "over 0.8 one in 54" at 50 days with ±1 steps. The research note's "71 %" was the *t-test* rejection rate, not P(R² > 0.8). So the bet's "over 0.8" is marked **"You called it"** on a screen whose best R² was 0.36 (screenshot F0-3). A fresher reads "right" next to 0.36 and learns that the page's verdicts are unreliable. Either lengthen the walks (R² of two random walks does not shrink with n, but the *lie* is in t, not R²) or re-ask the bet as "will the textbook test say 'real' (|t| > 2)?" — which is 70–98 % and *is* the shock. (b) Illustrative without caption? Yes: two wandering lines and a tilted cloud are self-explanatory. Better elsewhere: Tyler Vigen (https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations) gets the laugh first and the "non-independent observations … naive p-value overstates significance" reason second; the chapter has the Drunks' "ice cream and drownings" only as one line in F2. We miss the laugh.

**F1 — the honest fit (static).** The squares are the setosa move (https://setosa.io/ev/ordinary-least-squares-regression/) and they read without caption. But: the screenshot shows the formula clipped at "how-sure ≈ ±" (overflow), and the source has the same formula three times (`F1Honest.tsx` lines 41–43 — already known and being fixed; the clipping is the symptom to re-check after the fix). The leftover panel is 360×200 beside a 520×340 main plot; the "flat band of noise" is nine dots, which is not yet a *band*. Better elsewhere: Seeing Theory's OLS section (https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/regression-analysis/index.html) starts from **Anscombe's quartet** and lets you drag points; that gives "same line, four different clouds" in one gesture — the best possible opener for "the line is half the answer", and we don't have it anywhere.

**F2 — the four liars (comic).** The right mode for the body, and the comic surfaces are good. The *numbers on the panels* betray it. Funnel: "Your ± 0.31 is really ± 0.33" — a 6 % difference is not a lie, it is rounding. The cause: the fan is built symmetric about the centre (`0.3 + 2.2·|v − 5.5|`), where White and naive SEs nearly coincide. Jim Frost's cone (https://statisticsbyjim.com/regression/heteroscedasticity-regression/) fans one way and his sentence is "p-values that are smaller than they should be"; the storyboard's own line was "the SE badge says ±2 while the right-hand points scatter ±10". Make the fan one-sided and the ratio will be 2–3×. Echo's panel is the best of the four ("You counted 200 fresh coins; you had about 11") — that effective-sample-size sentence is better than anything in the outside sources. Twins: the circle diagram is the Analysis Factor move (https://theanalysisfactor.com/multicollinearity-explained-visually) but the panel text in the screenshot reads "the credit is 0 to me and 2 to my twin, each ± 10" — the storyboard promised "+50 / −49", which is the number that lands; 0 and 2 look like a mild disagreement. Drunks: screenshot shows "R² = 0.12"; the code now hunts for the first seed with R² > 0.8 — good, but a hunted pair should say so honestly ("pair number 37 that we tried"), which the code does. Illustrative without captions? Funnel and Echo yes; Twins no (two overlapping circles say "overlap", not "credit cannot be assigned"); Drunks no (a tilted cloud is just a tilted cloud until you are told the two series are strangers).

**3a — the clip.** Fifteen seconds of a residual line drawn in time order with a badge going green → amber. It is the right thing to animate and the beats are clean. But the x-axis label is "2016 → 2026, one point per trading day" and the series is **simulated** ("a bank index (simulated)") — the clip caption says nothing about that. A clip of a simulation presented as "the desk" is the one thing this library must not do. Also the clip and F3 step 3 draw the same thing twice within one scroll; the storyboard asked for the clip "followed by the pressable version", but on the page it is clip, then six steps of which step 3 is the clip again.

**F3 — the hedge that drifted (step-through + bet).** The strongest figure and the one closest to the note's Concept A. Drag-the-line, bet, watch, ACF bars, "use daily changes", three t-ratios — six steps is the most a fresher will take, and the StepThrough pane carries it. Faults: the residual plot's y-axis is in index points (±4,000) while the bet is on a slope ± (0.01 / 0.05 / 0.2) — two unrelated scales on one screen and no sentence connecting them. The "honest" ± is Newey–West with **L = 4** on 2,500 daily points whose leftovers "remember for months"; the result (2.16×) is admitted on-page to be "generous", i.e. the chapter's honest number is itself dishonest, and the learner's bet is *scored* against it. Tsay's rule gives L ≈ 8; for a unit-root residual no L is enough — which is the actual lesson (difference), and the scoring should be against the changes regression. The three-tile close has White's t (151.6) *above* textbook (147.6): "lets the leftovers fan out" producing a *more* confident number is the opposite of what the panel beside it claims. Illustrative without caption? Steps 1, 3, 5 yes; the ACF step needs its caption.

**F4 — fool it, then catch it (game).** Round 2's waffle grid ("98 in every 100 coin-pile pairs of 2,000 days pass the textbook test") is the single best illustration on the page — it is the r-bloggers table (https://www.r-bloggers.com/2019/06/spurious-correlations-and-random-walks/) turned into a picture, and it beats the source. Round 3's red "R² > DW → suspect" badge is the Estima rule made visible. Round 4 works, and the "noisy" class (real but low R², t still > 2) pre-empts "regression is useless" exactly as the note asked. Two faults: the three rounds hide behind a Segmented control a tired reader will not click (round 4 is the payoff, and it is two clicks away); and the "fooled N %" readout and the waffle say the same thing twice.

**F5 — the detective's card (break-it).** The card is good copy. The plot is not: the lower panel is residuals **in index order** for a cloud whose x is i.i.d. normal, so when "fan" is on the fan is invisible (screenshot F5-2: a flat noise band, White SE 0.09 vs naive 0.07). A fan is only visible as residual-vs-x. "runs" and "twins" together produce VIF 156 and the caption "coefficients 0.88 on x and −0.06 on its twin, with standard errors 0.63 and 0.63" — fine, but the Twins have no visual at all in this figure, only numbers. Four toggles plus "new cloud" plus seven readouts is the charter's "three sliders" problem in a different coat.

**F6 — sandbox.** Draggable setosa; the squares resize; good. But the readout "honest SE (Newey–West)" on **twelve cross-sectional points** in x-order is not a meaningful quantity: in both screenshots the "honest" SE is *smaller* than the naive one (0.04 vs 0.14; 0.16 vs 0.22), while the caption promises "the honest SE leaves the naive one behind". The sandbox contradicts its own caption on first load. Drop the HAC readout here, or make the sandbox a time-ordered one.

## 3. Immersion and simplicity — the fresher test

Where I would stop: **at F3 step 4** (the ACF). By then the reader has done a bet, a static, four comic panels, a 15-second clip, three steps, and now meets twenty bars labelled "memory of the leftovers" with a dashed 2/√n line that is never explained. The page is ~7,800 words of source including copy; the full-page screenshot is 8,200 px tall at 1100 wide — about four times chapter B4's weight for a topic the charter ranks *below* B4.

It is a template. B4's storyboard reads "bet → comic → explorable → step-through → guess+ledger → break-it"; A3's reads "game → static → comic → clip → step-through → game → break-it → sandbox" and the same four closing sections ("So what is …?", "Use it yourself", "Where it is used, every day", "The conclusion") with the same Ada/Mel interjections ("Pictures are easy to make up. Show me one that happened" / "So more data fixes it"). The charter wants pedagogy to vary per chapter; here only the nouns vary. The tell is that the chapter has *two* games (F0, F4) and *two* draggable scatters (F3 step 2, F6) and *two* drawings of the same residual series (clip, F3 step 3).

Too heavy where it should be light: the Newey–West formula appears in the open (`Newey–West = sqrt(Σu² + 2Σ_l(1−l/(L+1))Σu_t u_{t−l})/Σ(x−x̄)²`) in the "what computes the figures" panel — collapsed, fine — but the body also has "White", "Newey–West", "HAC", "Durbin–Watson", "VIF", "lag", "ACF", "log-returns" with none of them introduced before use. Too thin where it should be solid: R², SE, t, "textbook test" (see §1). The lightness amendment asks "would a tired fresher keep reading"; after F2 the answer is yes; after F3 I think no.

## 5. Ten changes, ranked by impact

1. **Fix the F0 bet so the verdict matches the screen.** Ask "will the textbook test call it real (|t| > 2)?" and score on that; it is 70 %+ and it is the lie. Drop "over 0.8" or show the honest "one in 54" *before* the bet.
2. **Resolve the slope contradiction.** Pick one message: "the slope is fine when the leftovers are a fan or an echo; it is *wrong* when both series wander" — and say it in the standfirst, F3 step 6 and the conclusion. Delete "The slope never changed."
3. **Add Anscombe's quartet as F1 (or F1b)** — four clouds, one line, drag any point (Seeing Theory). It gives outlier, leverage and curvature in one static and makes "the line is half the answer" literal.
4. **One collapsed panel explaining R², SE, ±2, t.** Four sentences, one picture (StatQuest's SS(mean) vs SS(fit)). Without it F0 and F4 are blind bets.
5. **Make the Funnel lie.** One-sided fan (sd ∝ x), so White/naive ≈ 2–3×; print the storyboard's "±2 vs ±10". In F5 plot residuals against x when "fan" is on, not in index order.
6. **Label the simulation in the clip and F3's first line**, or get the NIFTY BANK file before shipping. The clip currently says "2016 → 2026, one point per trading day" over invented data.
7. **Score F3's bet against the changes regression, not Newey–West L = 4.** Use Tsay's lag rule for the tile, and keep the on-page admission that HAC cannot rescue a unit-root residual — that *is* the lesson.
8. **Cut one of each duplicate**: the clip *or* F3 step 3; F6's HAC readout (meaningless on 12 cross-sectional points); the "fooled %" readout *or* the waffle. Target 5 figures, not 8.
9. **Promote F4 round 4 to the top of its figure** (the payoff is two clicks deep) and give the Twins a visual in F5 (two coefficient bars swinging between re-seeds).
10. **Give the Twins the +50/−49 numbers** the storyboard promised, and add the endogeneity sentence the note scheduled ("if the leftovers correlate with the input, the slope itself is wrong — chapter G1").

## 6. Verdict

The chapter picks the right hook, the right body mode (comic) and the right spine (a hedge ratio whose certainty evaporates when the leftovers are drawn in time order), and on spurious regression it beats every free resource I opened — the waffle of "98 in 100 pairs pass the test" is the best single frame on the page. But it is not yet trustworthy: the F0 bet congratulates a reader for a number the screen never showed; the Funnel panel's own numbers (0.31 vs 0.33) refute its caption; the sandbox's "honest" SE comes out *smaller* than the naive one; the slope is "usually fine" in paragraph one and doubles in Figure 3; and the real artefact is a simulation with the word "simulated" missing from the clip. It also leans on R², SE and t without ever teaching them, has no outlier, curve or leverage case (Anscombe), and runs the same bet → comic → clip → step-through → game → break-it → four-closers skeleton as B4 at four times B4's length. Fix the five number-vs-caption mismatches, add Anscombe and a four-line "what R² and t are" panel, cut the duplicates, and this becomes the best page on the topic I know of. As it stands a tired fresher stops at Figure 3 step 4, half-convinced and wrongly reassured.
