## 1. Numbers vs captions — the defects that break trust

These are the A3-class findings: the page says one thing and the screen shows another.

1. **C1 F5 contradicts itself on first load.** At the real 22,800 put (361.95) the head text says "That sits inside the put's bid–ask on the screen, 348.00 / 360.70 — not free money", and directly underneath, the ledger reads **"locked, whatever happens +6.40 a unit · ₹416.00"**, with the readouts "locked per unit +6.40 · per lot ₹416.00" and three check lines "index at 22,000 → +6.40". The engine is right (the real row is 6.40 off the median forward; `conversionLedger` reports it), but the page presents it as a riskless lock while saying it is not one. A fresher reads "₹416 free money at the real price". The ledger must stay empty (or read "inside the spread — nothing to lock") until the typed price leaves the bid–ask.
2. **B5 F1's "widen the range" silently changes the ticket.** F0 and F1 define the ticket as "pays 1 if the stock is 22, nothing if it is 18" — the words "right to buy at 21" never appear in B5 (only in C2). The toggle to 23 / 17 makes the ticket pay **2**, and the readout quietly says "23 or 17, pays 2 or 0". Raj's needle jumps 0.633 → 1.167 mainly because the payoff doubled, not because "how far the stock can swing sets the price" (a genuine pays-1-if-up ticket on the 23/17 board is worth 0.584 — it *falls*). The spine's one break-it move proves its sentence with a changed object. Either define the ticket as the right to buy at 21 from F0's prompt onward and say in F1 "on the wide board it pays 2", or keep the digital ticket and show the price falling.
3. **B5 F0's result card calls Priya's answer "Close."** Verdict thresholds are right < 0.03, close < 0.12, miss otherwise, so typing 0.70 — the number the whole chapter exists to refute, and which the same card says is "free money for Raj: 0.067 a ticket, ₹670 on a 10,000-lot" — earns "Close.", while 0.50 earns "miss". C2 F0 has the same shape (0.50 earns "Close."). The verdict should be "right" only at 0.63 ± 0.01 and otherwise "No — and the reason is a trade", because distance from 0.633 is not the lesson.
4. **B6's standfirst promises numbers the page never prints.** "They print 4.75 and 4.76 … and what the 0.01 is." The engine at seed 26 gives walkers **4.76 ± 0.05** and grid **4.76** (4.7595); F1, F3 and F4 all show 4.76 vs 4.76, gap 0.00. The 4.75 came from the storyboard's Python seed. Either print the engine's numbers in the standfirst or pick a seed that prints 4.75.
5. **C2 F3's observe is hard-wired to the call.** "0.5064 shares at the root, 0.7273 or 0 one step later" stays on screen when the reader switches to the put (root −0.4024, then −0.1667 / −1.0) and to the American put (readout "shares to hold there −0.5293" sits two lines above the observe's 0.5064). Make the sentence read the tree.
6. **B6 F1's clip is about a different option than the figure it lives in.** Beat 1's caption: "This rod is C2's 100-stock for a year; the grid beside it is Hull's 42." The storyboard asked for the rod to end with "a probe at 42 reading 4.76"; the clip's probe reads a 100-stock's price. The "watch / do" rule was adopted so the clip and the figure are one object; here they are two.
7. **B6 F1's tick and bracket disagree.** The drawn bracket on the rail is ±1 SE (`mc.lo … mc.hi`), the tick "✓ inside the bracket" uses `insideBracket(mc, value, z = 3)`. At seed 26 both agree, but a reseed could show the grid's dashed line outside the drawn bar with the tick still on.

Smaller: C1 F4's observe says "five hundredths of a percent" while its own ledger prints 0.056 %; C2's prose after F0 says "The tile did not move" to a reader who may not have locked a guess (the tile then shows "?"); B6 F2's "push" arrows are computed as the second difference in ln S (so they carry an S·∂V/∂S piece) while the label beneath says S²∂²V/∂S² — invisible to a fresher, worth one comment in the code.

Everything else checks: C1's seventeen implied forwards, median 22,603.75, min/max 22,596.60 / 22,609.30, 123.30 / 355.55 predicted puts, worst gap 6.40, +15.55 / ₹1,010.75 at a typed 340, break-even 22,849.15, ₹16,194.75 a lot, the 248.20 / 252.00 and 348.00 / 360.70 bid–asks (read off the artefact); C2's 0.633 / 0.6523 / 0.7041 / 42.56 % / 0.067 and 670, 1.2822 (book 1.2823, footnoted), 4.1927 / 5.0896 (book 4.1923 / 5.0894, footnoted), 12.1623 → 10.4486, 10.4506, 10.2534 / 10.6112, 10.4493; B5's 100.00 at θ = 0.25, 9.93 against 9.925, 7.15 % → 11.22 %, log-sd 0.20 at every θ, Z = 0.9265 / 1.1750, 1.40 / 1.06 / 0.80; B6's 1.25 vs 1.25000, 1.2507 ± 0.0122, weights 0.750 / −0.500 / 0.750, 1 vs 33 swings, 7,013.

## 9. Verdict

These four are the first chapters that read like the brief: a tired fresher would finish any of them. Each has a frame that is better than anything I found outside — C1's seventeen dots within thirteen points of one number, found by subtraction in a real NSE screen; C2's board tilting into a leaning bell with the sawtooth beside it; B5's two needles with faces, one of which will not move; B6's grid and walkers writing the same 4.76 into one box and then 1.25 against 1.25000. The lightness amendment has been honoured without the pages going thin: derivations are collapsed, the characters in B5 are legend swatches that earn every line, and the word counts are a third of A3's. On form, this is the standard the rest of the library should be built to.

What would make a sharp reviewer stop are the same kind of faults A3 had, in smaller number: C1's rebuild figure shows "₹416 locked, whatever happens" at the real price while telling the reader it is not free money; B5's one break-it move changes the ticket's payoff and calls the result "the range matters"; both bet chapters congratulate the wrong answer as "Close."; B6's standfirst quotes numbers the page never shows; and C2's first three figures are B5's first three figures with the faces removed. None of these is a redesign — items 1–5 are a day or two — but until they are done the pages say things their own numbers contradict, which is the one thing a page for engineers cannot do. Fix 1–5, draw C1's stack and B6's first frame, and I would put all four in front of the firm.
