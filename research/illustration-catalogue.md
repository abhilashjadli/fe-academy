## Part A — Design principles for a chapter

These are prescriptive. Each rule names the audit it came from.

### A1. Chapter anatomy

1. **Open with something touchable within the first 150 words.** Three patterns work: the finished object, draggable, with one sentence of hook (Ciechanowski's Mechanical Watch, Red Blob's A*); a puzzle the reader will get wrong (Taxi-cab 80 %, Entropy sheep, 3b1b's Steve); a playable game with a score (Kelly). Never open with definitions or a syllabus. **[longform]**
2. **Cadence: one figure per 150–250 words, 2–4 short paragraphs per figure.** Nobody in the benchmark set writes more than ~300 words without something to touch. The sentence immediately before a figure is an instruction or a claim the figure proves; the sentence after begins "Notice that…". **[longform; math-stat (Immersive Math captions)]**
3. **Build order: the naive version fails *in a figure* before the fix is named; each section adds exactly one component, rule or parameter; the sandbox with every lever comes last.** (Watch without escapement, GPS tape-measures → drones → sound, Polygons manual → automatic, L2 → L1.) **[longform]** Show the concrete case before the whole: one ball, one trial, one term, three points — then the flurry, the histogram, the series, the grid. **[3b1b 24-26 cross-cutting (c); 3b1b 21-23 Galton; 3b1b 19-20 T23]**
4. **Equations enter as picture → words → formula → consequence-figure.** 0–8 displayed equations per piece; the formula is named only after the reader has done the operation by hand (GPS clock bias, Kelly median, Bayes counts, 3b1b binomial "formula after the simulation agrees"). **[longform]** Every symbol is colour-locked to its picture part for the whole chapter, and tokens fly out of the picture into the formula; equation and picture are built in the same order, term by term. **[3b1b 19-20 T32; 3b1b 21-23 BuildUpGaussian; 3b1b 24-26 note (b)]**
5. **Every simulation ships with its measured companion panel** (histogram, error vs n on log axes, MSD vs t, breach count). The single biggest systematic gap in the simulation sites (Complexity Explorables especially); Distill and R Psychologist always have it. **[math-stat]** Any single-path view has a "run 1,000 of these" button and the distribution appears beside it — one path is an anecdote. **[quant §7.6]**
6. **"How this number was formed" is one click from every tile** (live substitution, riskhub EWMA style); units on every tile; everything renders with defaults, never empty-until-Calculate. **[quant §6, §7.2, §7.5, §7.9]**
7. **Time is a dimension, not a number.** Animate the price curve collapsing onto the payoff as t → T and Γ sharpening; nobody does. **[quant §7.7]**
8. **End with three one-line lessons, a "what computes it" code panel and a reading list — no bullet recap.** A scale-re-establishment figure sits near the end (Greeks of a real 1,000-contract position in currency; a 5σ day in index points). **[longform]**

### A2. Interaction rules

9. **Sliders for parameters, buttons for randomness, a fixed seed with an explicit "new sample" button.** A slider that re-seeds on every tick makes noise look like dependence. Speed control on every animation. **[math-stat presentation patterns; R Psychologist]** Pre-generate samples and reveal by index so playback is deterministic and reversible. **[3b1b 19-20 T21]**
10. **One slider per figure, two at most; the slider is a named physical quantity or time; the same scene is reused with one more control each time; complexity is controlled by pausing/slowing, not by adding controls.** **[longform, Ciechanowski]** Dials read as "something you turn" better than sliders for model parameters. **[3b1b 24-26 T24]**
11. **Button step-through (Back / Reset / Forward, "step 3 of 7") for derivations and algorithms.** Buttons beat scroll because the learner stops and rereads; scrollytelling only for a 3–6 step opener, never a whole chapter — quant readers read non-linearly. **[math-stat; Immersive Math, MLU-Explain]**
12. **Speed ramp for randomness:** three slow trials with the mechanism visible, then faster, then thousands; log-spaced reveal (10 / 100 / 1,000 / 10,000) is what makes 1/√N felt. **[3b1b 21-23 dice; 3b1b 24-26 T7; 3b1b 19-20 T21]**
13. **Drag-to-scrub on the chart, pin/ghost before–after, hover-the-curve-to-see-the-state as the default affordance on every time-series plot.** **[quant (drwong); math-stat (PAIR grokking)]**
14. **Predict-then-run:** the reader commits a bet, a guess or a sketch before every run; the wrong intuition is the hook. "You Draw It" for time series. **[longform (Trust, Simulating, Taxi-cab, rf-importance)]**
15. **A "break it" beat in nearly every chapter:** one honestly-labelled button does the wrong thing (p-hack, peek at the future, omit the −½σ² term, price below the bound); the honest number stays on screen; the message names *which invariant failed and shows the arithmetic*. Validate a diagnostic on a known-bad input first; show "turn it back" hysteresis where it exists. **[math-stat (R Psychologist, MLU, Berlin 8 a.m.); quant (blockchain, §7.4); longform (explained.ai)]**
16. **Two-model comparisons use common random numbers, identical axes, fixed colours per model, one shared control; the disagreement is the thing to notice.** **[quant §6; longform side-by-side]**
17. **Dim, don't delete; fade, don't remove; ghost the previous state so the eye measures the increment; morph the same objects, never redraw.** **[3b1b throughout: 19-20 T7, 21-23 wordle/continuous, 18 T10]**
18. **Small multiples with identical axes when the pattern across parameters is the point; three captioned stills instead of a slider when only three values matter** (the slider goes in an appendix). **[3b1b 24-26 T19; math-stat (Distill BO)]**
19. **3D only where the surface is the idea; always with a synchronised 2D slice, a colour legend and a slowly orbiting camera.** **[quant §7.8; 3b1b 18 T37; 3b1b 24-26 T12]**
20. **Every number on screen is bound to state; an annotated derivation re-flows as a whole when a parameter changes** (the ReconfigurableScene idea). **[3b1b 15-17 T38; 3b1b 24-26 note (a)]**

### A3. What the benchmark sites fail at — and we must do

- **No narrative, no "what to try first", no "what you should notice"** (drwong, riskhub, R Psychologist dashboards, Seeing Theory). Every explorable opens with a one-sentence prompt and closes with the observation stated. **[quant §7.1; math-stat]**
- **Intermediate steps hidden** (drwong never shows d₁ or derives p; CME never shows N(d₁)). **[quant §7.2]**
- **Tools do not connect**: the same S/K/T/σ/r panel should feed BS, tree(N), MC(N) and their differences; the same path should feed the hedging P&L and the backtest. **[quant §7.3]**
- **No misconceptions or counter-examples interactively** — only QuantEcon (periodic chains, Cauchy mean) and riskhub prose (OU Euler explosion, peeking weight, ρ = 0 smile) do it, never as a widget. **[quant §7.4]**
- **Undocumented units/ranges and arithmetic errors**: riskhub's Greeks page gives Δ = 0.6701 where its own BS page gives 0.6368; CME's worked P&L sums to 26.1 but says 18.1. Engines tested against the Python key; Hull/Natenberg numbers pinned in tests. **[quant §7.5, §7.11]**
- **Garbled maths in HTML** — KaTeX everywhere, tested. **[quant §7.10]**
- **Same widget shape everywhere** (Seeing Theory: button → accumulate → readout) teaches one lesson repeatedly. **[math-stat]**
- **Free-play widgets placed after the explanation**, so the learner never predicts; **stale text vs figure** (MLU). **[math-stat]**
- **Simulation without measurement** (Complexity Explorables: "grows like √N" asserted, never plotted). **[math-stat]**
- **Nothing visual exists on exotics, structured products, measure change, Itô, quadratic variation or Feynman-Kac.** B2–B6 and H1–H2 are open ground (see Part D). **[quant §7.12–13; longform (no 3b1b lesson on random walks/finance)]**

### Family 2 — Continuum limits & zoom

#### B10. Secant → tangent with dt printed — and its failure on a Brownian path
A dx-line and df-line form a right triangle on the graph; dx is interpolated toward 0 while the secant rotates into the tangent and the ratio prints as a changing decimal; the 3b1b lesson does this on t³ at t = 2 (ratio 3(2)² + 3(2)dt + dt² → 12). Run the same widget on a sampled Brownian path and the slope ratio does *not* settle (it grows like 1/√dt) while Σ(ΔX)² does. Makes visible: a limit is "approach", not "plug in zero"; and the one fact B3 rests on.
