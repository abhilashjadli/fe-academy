## 1. The one thing the learner must come away feeling

A bucket on a Galton board is a **sum of coin flips**. Counting the paths into each bucket gives Pascal's triangle and the binomial distribution; rescale the binomial — or the sum of *any* dice with finite variance — and the same bell appears every time. Probability is **area under that bell**, not height. In this firm: a day's return is a sum of many small shocks, which is why the normal is everywhere in finance — and the Galton board turned on its side is the price lattice you will price options on in C2.

Secondary: why C(n, k) = n!/(k!(n−k)!) (choosing *which* k bounces go right); mean and standard deviation of a sum (sd grows like √n); the 68–95–99.7 rule; z-scores; the three assumptions the bell needs (independence, identical-enough, finite variance) and what happens when each fails.

## 2. Baseline check

Assumes: random variables, expectation, variance, independence (charter §2). Does *not* assume combinatorics or the normal density — both are taught here, as the sponsor asked for strong illustrations of them. Chapter is standalone; A1 continues it (lognormal, fat tails) and C2 reuses the lattice.

## 4. Chosen form and why

**Experiment with a step-through spine.** The chapter is about *watching counts become a shape*, so the carrier is simulation with the mechanism visible: one ball slowly, then three, then a flurry, then 10,000 — the speed ramp. Two step-throughs sit inside it where the maths enters (counting paths → Pascal → C(n,k); building the Gaussian). One sandbox (the die editor). Three "break it" buttons. The finance payoff is a rotation of the opening figure, not a new figure. Randomness is pre-generated with a fixed seed and an explicit "new sample" button; sliders never re-seed.

Size: 10 figures, ~2,300 words, 5 displayed equations.

## 5. Storyboard

**F0 — The board.** A live Galton board (12 rows, ~25 buckets), empty. One button: *Drop 20 balls.* Before it: *Place your bet — which bucket fills highest?* (click a bucket to bet). Then drop. Caption after: *No bounce is predictable; the pile is.* Second button: *Drop 500* (speed ramp, clink sounds optional). (T1.)

**F1 — One ball, slowly (step-through, one step per row).** The board fades to a 5-row triangle. A ball falls one peg per click; at each peg two arrows, −1 and +1; the chosen one stays bright; a running sum in the corner: +1, 0, +1, +2, +1. It lands in the bucket labelled **+1**. Caption: *The bucket is the sum of five ±1 flips.* Repeat for two more balls with "auto" speed. (T1.)

**F2 — Counting the ways in (step-through).** Buckets now labelled by k = number of right-bounces (0…5). Click bucket k = 2: all 10 paths light up. The pegs acquire fractions row by row — each parent's fraction splits into its two children and the children's fractions add (1 → ½ ½ → ¼ ½ ¼ → …), Pascal's triangle over 2ⁿ. Caption: *the number of paths into bucket k is the number of ways to choose which 2 of the 5 bounces go right.* (T2.)

**F3 — Choosing k of n.** A row of five coins, all tails. Click any two to make them heads: a path on the small board beside it draws itself (heads = right). A counter of distinct selections climbs to 10 as the learner tries them; a "show all" button fans out all ten. Then the count *by hand*: 5 choices for the first head, 4 for the second, ÷2 because order doesn't matter = 10. Only now: **C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n−k)!)**, with 5!/(2!3!) = 10 evaluated beside it. (T3.)

**F4 — The binomial as bars.** Brick rows: for each path a brick; bricks copy-shift-stack into a histogram over k. Slider n (1…100) and slider p (peg bias, 0.05…0.95; the board visibly tilts). Bars re-form; live readouts mean = np and sd = √(np(1−p)); the formula **P(k) = C(n,k) pᵏ (1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ** appears *after* the bars, colour-locked (C(n,k) = number of paths, pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ = probability of one path). Ghost of n = 10 kept as n grows so the eye sees the bell forming. (T4.)

**F5 — Not just coins: any die.** A die editor: six bars the learner drags to make an unfair die (default: weighted toward 1 and 2, so the start is visibly skewed). Beside it, histograms of the sum of n = 1, 2, 5, 15, 50 dice, built by simulation with the speed ramp (three slow rolls with the dice visible, then thousands). For n = 2 a small 6×6 grid shows the exact count by anti-diagonals (where the counting of F2 reappears). Button *standardise*: subtract the mean, divide by the sd — the five histograms slide and squeeze onto one curve. Readouts: μ, σ², σ√n. Caption: *whatever die you drew, the standardised sum lands on the same curve.* Predict-then-run: before standardising, *sketch where the n = 50 hump will be.* (T5 + T6.)

**F6 — The curve (animated derivation, Back/Reset/Forward, 6 steps; Manim clip permitted if the in-browser morph is weaker).** Start from e^{−x²} (a hump); widen with σ: e^{−x²/(2σ²)}; shift with μ; the area under it is σ√(2π), so divide by it; final **f(x) = 1/(σ√2π) · e^{−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)}**, each symbol lighting the part of the graph it controls. Then the sentence the whole chapter turns on: **area, not height, is probability** — shade an interval, show the number. (T7.)

**F7 — The rule of thumb.** The standard bell with two draggable bounds; shaded area readout; snap points at ±1σ, ±2σ, ±3σ reading 68.27%, 95.45%, 99.73%. A z-score readout for any point: *how many sds from the mean*. (T8.)

**F8 — Measure it: 100 fair dice.** Readouts from the formulas: mean 350, sd 17.08, 95% band 316.5–383.5. Predict-then-run: *what is the chance the total exceeds 400?* (learner types a guess). Button *roll 100 dice × 10,000 times*: histogram grows (log-spaced reveal 10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000); breach counter for > 400 settles near 0.16%; the normal says 0.155%. Companion panel: observed vs predicted band coverage. Second check with coins: n = 100, p = ½, P(k ≥ 60) exact 2.84% vs bell 2.87%. (T9.)

**F9 — Break it (three buttons on the board from F0).** *Sticky pegs* (a ball that went right is more likely to go right again — dependence): the pile spreads and flattens, sd no longer √n. *Uneven pegs* (each row biased differently — not identical): still a bell; the theorem is tolerant. *One wild face* (a die with a rare face worth 1,000 — heavy tail): the standardised sums never settle; the running sd keeps jumping. Each button shows the honest numbers next to the broken ones. Caption: *independence and finite variance are what the bell is made of — A1 is about markets, where both are suspect.* (T10.)

**F10 — Payoff: turn the board on its side.** The F0 board rotates 90°: pegs become a lattice, time runs left to right, each bounce is a day's up/down move, a single ball's path is a price history, and the buckets on the right are the distribution of where the price ends. Button *drop 500* repopulates it sideways. Caption: *this lattice is the binomial tree; in C2 you will price an option on it. In A1 the steps become percentages and the bell becomes the lognormal.* (T11.)

**Ending.** One paragraph: the sentence from §1; "what computes it" panel (the 8 lines that simulate the board and the 3 that evaluate the binomial); links: A1, A2 (what happens with two correlated boards), C2, E2 (the √n again, as Monte Carlo error).

## 6. Mathematics (for the engine and tests)

- Paths into bucket k of an n-row board: C(n, k). Pascal recursion C(n, k) = C(n−1, k−1) + C(n−1, k).
- Binomial: P(k) = C(n,k) pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ; mean np; variance np(1−p).
- Sum of n i.i.d. X: mean nμ, variance nσ², sd σ√n. Standardised sum (S − nμ)/(σ√n) → N(0,1) (CLT; finite variance required).
- Normal density f(x) = (σ√2π)⁻¹ exp(−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)); ∫e^{−x²/2}dx = √2π.
- Exact distribution of a sum of two dice by convolution: P(s) = Σ p(x)p(s−x) (the anti-diagonal count).
- Continuity correction for the normal approximation to a discrete sum (use it in F8 and say so in one line).

## 7. Reference numbers for tests (verified with Python 2026-10-21)

- C(5,2) = 10; 2⁵ = 32; P(k = 2) = 0.3125.
- Fair die: μ = 3.5, σ² = 35/12 = 2.91667, σ = 1.70783. 100 dice: mean 350, sd 17.0783; 95% band 316.53–383.47; P(sum > 400) ≈ 0.00155 (normal, continuity-corrected).
- Coins n = 100, p = 0.5: P(k ≥ 60) exact 0.028444; normal with continuity correction 0.028717.
- 68–95–99.7: 0.6827, 0.9545, 0.9973.
- Pascal row 5 over 32: 1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1.
- Simulation seeds fixed; histogram of 10,000 × 100-dice sums must put 95.0–95.9% of mass inside the band (tolerance for the test).

## 8. Misconceptions to address

- "The bell is about coins / symmetric things" → F5 starts from a skewed die.
- "Probability is the height of the curve" → F6's sentence and F7's shading.
- "More trials make each outcome more likely to be average" → no: the *sum* spreads like √n; the *mean* tightens like 1/√n (show both readouts in F8).
- "Averages of anything become normal" → F9's wild-face die.
- "C(n,k) is a formula to memorise" → F3 counts it by hand first.
