# 0.1 — Taylor series: "derivatives at one point buy you the curve nearby"

_Phase R1 research note + storyboard. Status: DRAFT for sponsor taste-check. Built from: 3Blue1Brown Essence of Calculus ch. 10/11 (scene code `_2017/eoc/chapter10.py`, written lesson), Hull OFOD 5e Appendix 14A (p. 329), audits in `research/3b1b/*` and `research/sites/*`._

## 1. The one thing the learner must come away feeling

Knowing a function's value, slope, curvature, … **at a single point** lets you rebuild the function **in a neighbourhood of that point**, one term per derivative — and the error is governed by the first term you left out. In this firm that sentence reads: *a position's P&L for a market move δS is Δ·δS + ½Γ·δS² + …; every Greek is a Taylor coefficient.*

Secondary: the factorial in each term is not decoration — it is what makes each coefficient "own" exactly one derivative. And: more terms is not always better (radius of convergence).

## 2. Baseline check

Audience knows derivatives, chain rule, power rule, partial derivatives (charter §2). Has forgotten Taylor series. No finance assumed: the option-value curve in the last figure is introduced as "a curved asset value" only; nothing about options is needed. Chapter is standalone.

## 4. Chosen form and why

**Step-through animated explorable with two sandboxes.** The derivation is inherently sequential (value, then slope, then curvature; each step locks one knob), so a Back/Reset/Forward timeline with synchronised captions is the right carrier — it does the handholding. It is bracketed by two free sandboxes: the knobs (before, discovery) and the n-slider build-up with a draggable centre (after, consolidation). The convergence failure is a scripted "break it" beat. The finance ladder closes the loop opened in the first figure. No narration in v1; captions are written to be read aloud later.

Approximate size: 9 figures, ~2,000 words of prose, 6 equations (all introduced *after* their picture, per the long-form anatomy in `sites/longform-and-3b1b-lessons.md`).

## 5. Storyboard

**F0 — Opening (static + one slider).** A smooth curved graph labelled "value of a position vs the market level S". A dot at today's S. Slider: "market moves by δS". Two guesses drawn: a straight line through the dot, and a parabola. Caption: *Every risk report in this firm is a short polynomial. By the end of this page you'll know why, and why the second term has a ½ in it.* The equation δΠ = Δ·δS + ½Γ·δS² + … is shown greyed-out; it un-greys in F8.

**F1 — Sandbox: hug the curve.** cos x in blue on [−3, 3]. Polynomial c₀ + c₁x + c₂x² in yellow with three knobs (ranges −2…2, step 0.05). Live readouts: value of both at x = 0.1 and at x = 1, and the absolute error. Prompt: *make the yellow curve indistinguishable from the blue one near x = 0.* Most people find c₀ = 1 quickly, c₁ = 0 by feel, and hover around c₂ ≈ −0.5. (Technique T1.)

**F2 — Step-through: do it properly (Back / Reset / Forward, 7 steps).**
1. At x = 0 the polynomial equals c₀; cos 0 = 1 → c₀ = 1. Knob c₀ locks (turns grey).
2. Draw the tangent to cos at 0 (flat). Derivative of cos is −sin; −sin 0 = 0.
3. Derivative of the polynomial is c₁ + 2c₂x; at 0 it is c₁ → c₁ = 0. Knob locks.
4. Curvature: cos bends *down* at 0; second derivative of cos is −cos; −cos 0 = −1.
5. Second derivative of the polynomial is 2c₂ (the 2 comes from the power rule, shown) → c₂ = −½. Knob locks. Boxed: **1 − x²/2**.
6. Check with a number: cos(0.1) = 0.995004…; 1 − 0.005 = 0.995. Brace-and-decimals readout.
7. Caption: *Three facts at one point — height, slope, bend — gave us a curve that works nearby.* (T2.)

**F3 — Why factorials: each coefficient owns one derivative.** Add c₃x³ and c₄x⁴. A 5×5 table: rows = terms 1, x, x², x³, x⁴; columns = 0th…4th derivative evaluated at 0. Animate differentiating x⁴ four times: 4·3·2·1 = 24 appears; every off-diagonal entry is 0. So the k-th derivative at 0 is cₖ·k! and nothing else — *higher terms never disturb lower ones.* Only now the general coefficient: cₖ = f⁽ᵏ⁾(0)/k!. (T3.)

**F4 — Main explorable: build it term by term.** Function selector: cos x | eˣ | ln x | √(1+x) | the asset curve from F0. Stepper n = 0…10 ("add a term"). Previous polynomial ghosted; current in yellow; shaded band |f − Pₙ|; formula prefix boxed with the term just added highlighted; draggable x-probe showing f(x), Pₙ(x), error. (T4.)

**F5 — Drag the centre.** Same figure; the expansion point a is a draggable dot on the curve; Pₙ re-centres with (x − a)ᵏ; the band moves with it. Caption: *the series knows the function near wherever you ask.* General formula f(x) ≈ Σ f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)/k! · (x − a)ᵏ appears here, after the picture. (T5.)

**F6 — Does it ever stop? Convergence and its radius.** Left: eˣ at x = 1, partial sums 1, 2, 2.5, 2.667, 2.708, 2.717, 2.7181, 2.71825… settling under a brace onto 2.71828. Right: ln x about a = 1, with an x-slider. At x = 1.5 the partial sums settle (0.5, 0.375, 0.4167, 0.4010, 0.4073 → 0.4055). Drag to x = 2.5: the terms grow — the sums bounce ever wider. The interval (0, 2] lights up on the axis: *radius of convergence = 1.* Break-it beat: "more terms made it worse". (T6.)

**F7 — Zoom, then measure.** Pick a point on cos x; zoom ×10 — the curve and the tangent line coincide (first order is enough); zoom back out — the gap is a parabola (second order). Companion panel: log-log plot of |error| vs δx for P₀, P₁, P₂, with measured slopes 1, 2, 3. Caption: *halve the move and the first-order error quarters.* This is the Lagrange remainder shown, not stated; the formula Rₙ = f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(ξ)/(n+1)! · (x − a)ⁿ⁺¹ is given in a footnote. (T7 + T8.)

**F8 — The payoff: a risk report is a Taylor series.** The asset curve from F0 is a European call value V(S) (parameters fixed and shown: S = 100, K = 100, r = 5%, σ = 20%, T = 1 — "you'll learn where this curve comes from in C3"). δS slider −30…+30. Three overlays toggled on in order: Δ·δS (line), + ½Γ·δS² (parabola), exact. Table: δS, Δ-only, Δ+½Γ, exact. Equation un-greys: δΠ = Δ·δS + ½Γ·δS² + Θ·δt + …, with Δ = slope, Γ = curvature named as *the Greeks* (link C4) and Θ·δt as the time term (link D1). Final caption: *In B4 you'll meet a world where the second-order term refuses to vanish even for tiny steps — that is where this chapter and stochastic calculus meet.* (T9.)

**Ending.** One paragraph, no bullet recap: the sentence from §1, then links: 0.2 (not needed), C4 Greeks I, D1 hedging P&L, B4 Itô.

## 6. Mathematics (for the engine and tests)

- Taylor polynomial: Pₙ(x; a) = Σₖ₌₀ⁿ f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)/k! · (x − a)ᵏ. Lagrange remainder Rₙ = f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(ξ)/(n+1)! · (x − a)ⁿ⁺¹ for some ξ between a and x.
- Functions and derivatives supplied analytically: cos, exp, ln (about a > 0), √(1+x). The asset curve V(S) is Black-Scholes call with analytic Δ = N(d₁), Γ = φ(d₁)/(Sσ√T); the engine only needs V, V′, V″ as callables — no option theory is taught here.
- Radius of convergence of ln x about 1 is 1 (series in (x − 1)); of eˣ, cos x infinite.

## 8. Misconceptions to address in the text

- "The polynomial fits the curve" → it fits *at a point*; the fit decays with distance (F4 band).
- "More terms always help" → not outside the radius (F6).
- "The ½ in ½Γ δS² is a convention" → it is 1/2! from matching the second derivative (F3).
- "Taylor ≈ regression" → no data, no least squares; it is a local identity of derivatives.
- Engineers often remember the formula but not *why* each term is independent — F3 is the fix.
