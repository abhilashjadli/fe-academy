## 2. Technique catalogue

### T1. Flickering random value on a number line with a fading trail
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2024/puzzles/max_rand.py` (`Randomize`, `TrackingDots`, `MaxProcess`, `SqrtProcess`); same idiom in `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/spheres_talk/random_puzzles.py` (`IntervalWithSample`, `DotHistory`, `randomize_intervals`) and `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2024/puzzles/added_dimension.py` (`ProbabilityQuestion`).
- Topic illustrated there: what `rand()` means; max of two uniforms; √ of a uniform.
- What moves / what the viewer does: a `ValueTracker` is re-rolled 4–8 times a second; an arrow tip jumps along a `UnitInterval`, a live decimal reads the value, and a glow dot is left behind at each position with opacity decaying ×0.95 per frame so a density picture accumulates. A derived quantity (max, sqrt, Σx²) has its own line with its own trail and a dashed line back to the input that produced it. Tag: `sampling/simulation`.
- What it makes visible that text cannot: that a random variable *is* a process plus a density; that a transformation of a random variable reshapes where the dots pile up (√x pushes mass right; max pushes mass right in exactly the same way).
- Our chapters that could use it: 0.2 (what a random variable is, before any formula), A1 (normal → lognormal: trail on x and on e^x side by side), E2 (inverse-transform sampling: u → F⁻¹(u) with two trails), C1 (payoff max(S−K,0) as a transformed trail), H1 (lookback = max of a path).
- Notes on execution: the trail opacity decay is the whole trick — it turns a point process into a density without ever saying "histogram". Frequency ~4 Hz, run 10–30 s. Keep the live decimal readout; it anchors the flicker to a number. Pitfall: with too fast a flicker the eye stops reading values and only sees the density; use two speeds.

### T2. Two random numbers become one random point; "≤ R" becomes an area
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2024/puzzles/max_rand.py` (`VisualizeMaxOfPairCDF`); `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/spheres_talk/random_puzzles.py` (`SumOfTwoSquares`).
- Topic illustrated there: P(max(x₁,x₂) ≤ R) = R²; P(x²+y² ≤ 1) = π/4.
- What moves: the two number lines rotate into the x- and y-axes of a square (`Transform(number_line, axes.x_axis)`); the pair becomes a dot with dashed drop-lines; trail becomes a 2D scatter; the event {max ≤ 0.7} is drawn as an L-shaped boundary and then filled as a square by shrinking copies of the L inward (a `LaggedStart` of 100 scaled copies); finally a z-axis is written and the square extrudes into a cube. Tag: `geometric-reinterpretation`.
- What it makes visible: a CDF is an area (volume) in sample space; independence = uniform fill of the square; "and" = intersection of strips.
- Our chapters: 0.2 (joint probability as area), A2 (correlation as non-uniform fill of the square; the circle-in-square as a 2D Gaussian contour), E1 (VaR as a quantile boundary cutting a cloud; ES as the mass beyond it), E2 (hit-or-miss Monte Carlo for π = acceptance ratio), H1 (worst-of basket = min of coordinates: the L-shape again).
- Notes: the number-line-to-axis `Transform` is the moment of understanding — keep it slow (2 s). Extruding to 3D is cheap in Three.js and pays off for "three assets".

### T3. n sliders, a live running sum, and a ✓/✗ that flips
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2024/puzzles/added_dimension.py` (`ProbabilityQuestion`), `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/spheres_talk/random_puzzles.py` (`AskAboutLargeSumOfSquares`, `get_evaluation_object`).
- Topic: P(x₁²+…+x_n² ≤ 1) for uniforms; volume of the n-ball collapses with n.
- What moves: 6 (or 100, with ⋮) sliders re-roll; an equation `(+0.00)² + (+0.00)² + … = 1.00` has every number bound to a tracker (`make_number_changeable` + `f_always.set_value`); a checkmark/exmark toggles opacity on the threshold. Tag: `sampling/simulation`.
- What it makes visible: concentration of measure — as n grows the ✓ almost never appears; the "volume" formula becomes a felt frequency.
- Our chapters: A2 (Mahalanobis distance / χ² with n degrees of freedom; why a 10-asset portfolio's return vector is never near the origin), E1 (Monte Carlo VaR as counting ✓ among trials), G2 (curse of dimensionality for clustering), E2 (acceptance-rejection efficiency).
- Notes: the equation whose numbers are live is more convincing than a chart; show the ✓ count out of N trials as a running fraction.

### T4. A histogram that morphs as a dimension slider runs 2 → 1000
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2024/puzzles/added_dimension.py` (`RandomVectorStatistics`, `get_random_angle_data_histogram`).
- Topic: angle between two random unit vectors concentrates at 90° in high dimension.
- What moves: a `dim_tracker` animates with `rush_into`; on every frame the chart is rebuilt (`chart.become(histogram(dim))`) from 50M/dim fresh samples; the bin width halves when dim ≥ 100; the dimension label counts up. Tag: `sampling/simulation` + `zoom/continuum-limit`.
- What it makes visible: a distribution as a function of a parameter — the whole shape sharpening — rather than one frozen histogram per n.
- Our chapters: 0.2 (binomial → normal as n grows; CLT with the sample size as the slider), A1 (Student-t ν → ∞; kurtosis), A2 (sample correlation of independent series as dimension/length grows), C2 (binomial tree n → Black–Scholes), E2 (MC estimate distribution narrowing as 1/√N).
- Notes: precompute histograms for a grid of n and interpolate; recomputing in the browser is fine for ≤1e5 samples. Rescale the y-axis gently (they take √ of the overflow) so the peak growing does not blow the axis.

### T5. Random-walk rollouts with success/failure colouring
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/RL/rollouts.py` (`RandomWalks`).
- Topic: RL rollouts hitting reward regions.
- What moves: 20 paths of 50 steps with normally-distributed step angles are drawn with `ShowCreation` staggered by `lag_ratio=1/n`; a glow dot rides each tip; afterwards paths that ended inside a region turn green and thicken, the rest fade to 10 % opacity. Tag: `sampling/simulation`.
- What it makes visible: an ensemble; the *fraction* of paths meeting a condition; the asymmetry between "almost hit" and "hit".
- Our chapters: B1 (random walks, first-passage), B2 (martingale: average of path endpoints), E2 (MC pricing = colour the paths that pay), H1 (barrier/knock-in as "touch the region", digital as end-in-region), F2 (Longstaff–Schwartz: paths that exercise early), D1 (hedging P&L paths).
- Notes: stagger the drawing so the viewer sees randomness unfold; recolouring *after* the fact is the pedagogical move — first they guess, then the answer is revealed. Keep n ≈ 20–50 for a story, 1000+ only as faint background.

### T6. Ensemble of nearly identical initial conditions diverging (with fading tails)
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2024/manim_demo/lorenz.py` (`LorenzAttractor`); the tail idiom `TracingTail(dot, time_traced=3)`.
- Topic: sensitivity to initial conditions.
- What moves: 10 ODE solutions started 1e-5 apart, drawn at linear rate with dots and 3-second tails, camera slowly orbiting. Tag: `animate-the-object` + `sampling/simulation`.
- What it makes visible: deterministic ≠ predictable; the moment of separation.
- Our chapters: A4 (random walk vs mean reversion: bundle of AR(1) paths from one x₀, φ slider), B3 (OU paths funnel, GBM paths fan), E2 (variance of MC estimators), G3 (backtest overfitting — tiny changes in training window, wildly different "optimal" strategies).
- Notes: tails must be short (2–3 s) or the screen fills; colour the bundle with a gradient so individual paths stay identifiable.

### T7. Live Monte-Carlo histogram with a trial counter
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/monthly_mindbenders/ladybug.py` (`Simulation`); `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/monthly_mindbenders/chords.py` (`ChordQuestion`, intersection counts for 10 then 100 chords); `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2026/monthly_mindbenders/strings.py`.
- Topic: random walk on a clock face (which number is visited last?); expected chord intersections.
- What moves: each trial runs invisibly, the single result is shown (bug lands on the clock number), then `hist.animate.become(new_hist)` with proportions; a `Trial: n` label increments. Tag: `sampling/simulation`.
- What it makes visible: convergence of a frequency to a probability, and — in the clock puzzle — a *flat* histogram that nobody expected.
- Our chapters: 0.2 (Galton board: the same trial-by-trial build), B1 (Markov chain hitting distributions), E1 (backtesting exceptions count), E2 (convergence; show the running estimate ± 1/√N band).

### T8. Bell curve by copy–shift–stack
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/outside_videos/probability.py` (`StacksApproachBellCurve`).
- Topic: repeated convolution with a fair coin.
- What moves: the current bar set is copied, the copy slides right by one bin, lands on top of the original (`MoveToTarget` with lag), then bars are merged and rescaled to fit; 70 iterations; colours run blue→yellow by bin. Tag: `build-up`.
- What it makes visible: the binomial is a *recursion*, and the bell shape emerges from nothing but "shift and add".
- Our chapters: 0.2 (Galton board / Pascal's triangle / CLT in one object), C2 (binomial tree: the stack *is* the terminal distribution of the tree), B1 (random walk distribution after n steps).
- Notes: let the viewer press "step" for the first 5 iterations and then "run"; rescaling width/height each step is what keeps it on screen — do it with a short tween.

### T17. Taylor terms as chained arrows in the complex plane, with a t-slider
- Source: `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/_2025/laplace/exponentials.py` (`ImaginaryInputsToTheTaylorSeries`, `get_spiral_vectors`); same idiom in `/home/claude/3b1b/videos/outside_videos/analysis.py` (`SumRotVectors`, `ZetaSpiral`).

## 3. Top 10 ideas for our chapters

1. **Expectation as area (T21 + T20)** — one widget for 0.2, A1, C1, C3, E1, G1, B5: a stacked-width bar for probabilities, bars for values, the total area read off a ruler; "slosh" it flat to show the mean. Girsanov as re-widening segments with heights pinned is the cleanest change-of-measure picture we have.
2. **Dice → point → area (T1 + T2)** for 0.2/A2/E1: flickering values with fading trails, number lines rotate into axes, "≤ R" becomes a shaded region, extrude to 3D for a third asset. Also the inverse-transform sampling picture for E2.
3. **Stretch the ellipse into a circle (T14)** for A2 Cholesky/whitening and its inverse (build correlated samples), with the physical scatter and the abstract plane synchronised.
4. **Drag the poles (T18)** for A4 stationarity (AR(2) roots vs the unit circle with the impulse response bound to them), A5 GARCH persistence, B3 OU half-life.
5. **Taylor terms as chained arrows / stacked bars added one at a time (T17)** for 0.1, and the same build-up for delta-gamma-speed in C4/C5 and for Itô in B4.
6. **Masked dot-grid matrix (T26)** for G3 leakage ("the model cannot see the future" as a blanked triangle) and for A2/E3 correlation matrices.
7. **Rollouts coloured by outcome (T5)** for E2/H1: paths drawn with a stagger, then those that pay (barrier touched, digital in-region, early exercise) light up; the price is the green fraction.
8. **Grid morph under exp (T29)** for A1 lognormal and B3 GBM: warp the normal grid and its density together; same warp machinery for D2 log-moneyness and A2 Cholesky.
9. **Histogram morphing with a parameter slider (T4) + copy-shift-stack (T8)** for 0.2 CLT/Galton and C2 binomial→Black–Scholes: the shape sharpening as n runs, not four frozen snapshots.
10. **Loss landscape with a walking model icon (T23) + dials (T24)** for A3/A5/F2/G1: show the loss as a single number on an axis first, then reveal the bowl; parameters as dials, not sliders; "break it" by adding collinearity or heteroskedastic noise and watching the bowl flatten into a valley.

Cross-cutting execution notes: (a) every number on screen is bound to a tracker (`make_number_changeable` + updater) — nothing is typed in; (b) build the equation and the picture *in the same order, term by term*; (c) show the concrete case (three points, one trial, one term) before the whole (grid, histogram, series); (d) reveal the second linked view with a camera move after the first has been played with; (e) fading trails and small multiples are the two cheapest ways to turn a single instance into a distribution.
