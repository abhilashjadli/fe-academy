## 2. Technique catalogue

### T1. Polynomial approximation built term by term, hugging the curve
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter10.py` — `ExampleApproximation`, `ExampleApproximationWithSine`, `ExampleApproximationWithExp`, `ExpGraphConvergence`, `CubicAndQuarticApproximations`
- Topic illustrated there: Taylor series of e^{−x²}, sin x, e^x, cos x
- What moves / what the viewer does: the true function is drawn; a green approximation graph starts as a single point at the expansion centre and is `Transform`ed into the degree-0, then degree-2, then degree-4 polynomial; each time the matching term in the formula fades from transparent to full colour. In `ExpGraphConvergence` a brace under the written series grows one term at a time while the graph morphs and a dot at x=2 climbs toward e².
- What it makes visible that text cannot: the approximation "hugging" the curve over a widening neighbourhood as terms are added; the per-term correspondence between symbol and shape; the fact that the fit is local to the centre.
- Our chapters that could use it: **0.1** — the core illustration (slider or step-through over degree n, with the matching term highlighted). **C4/C5** — delta-gamma Taylor expansion of option price in S: show the P&L curve and its 1st/2nd-order approximations the same way. **B4** — Itô's lemma is a 2nd-order Taylor expansion; same visual with the dx² term kept. **D1** — gamma P&L ≈ ½Γ(ΔS)², shown as the parabola hugging the payoff.
- Notes on execution: colour-code each term and its graph; transform the *same* graph object so the eye follows continuity; mark the centre with a dashed vertical line; keep the y-range fixed so divergence away from the centre is visible. Tag: **build-up**.

### T2. Free-coefficient polynomial matched to a curve one derivative at a time
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter10.py` — `ConstructQuadraticApproximation` (sub-steps `introduce_quadratic_constants`, `let_c1_and_c2_vary`, `show_tangent_slope`, `show_matching_curvature`), `EachTermControlsOneDerivative`, `HigherTermsDontMessUpLowerTerms`
- Topic illustrated there: why Taylor coefficients are f^{(n)}(0)/n!
- What moves / what the viewer does: a quadratic c₀+c₁x+c₂x² is drawn next to cos x with the three constants labelled "free to change"; the graph is re-drawn for several (c₀,c₁,c₂) triples; then c₀ is pinned (value at 0), the tangent line is drawn and c₁ pinned (slope), then c₂ is varied until the curvature matches. Arrows from each cₙ to "controls dⁿP/dxⁿ(0)".
- What it makes visible that text cannot: that each coefficient is a knob controlling exactly one derivative at the centre, and that later knobs do not disturb earlier matches.
- Our chapters that could use it: **0.1** — an explorable with three knobs where the learner tries to fit cos x by hand before being shown the rule (break-the-model / discovery). **A5** — fitting a parametric model by hand before MLE. **G1** — polynomial regression with degree as a knob (links to over-fitting).
- Notes on execution: this is the natural "sliders" chapter; make the knobs discrete steps with a visible residual so the learner feels the matching. Tag: **animate-the-object** (parameter explorable).

### T3. Partial sums with a growing brace and a running decimal
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter10.py` — `ConvergenceExample`, `ExpConvergenceExample`; `_2015/inventing_math.py` — `SeeNumbersApproachOne`, `ShowDecreasingDistance`; `_2017/crypto.py` — `ShowGeometricSum`
- Topic illustrated there: convergence of Σ1/3ⁿ, Σ1/n!, block-reward halving 50+25+12.5+…
- What moves / what the viewer does: a brace under the first n terms of a written series stretches one term at a time with the partial sum printed to 7 decimals; or dots at the partial sums march along a number line, shrinking as they crowd the limit.
- What it makes visible that text cannot: the *rate* of convergence (digits stabilising), and the gap to the limit shrinking geometrically.
- Our chapters that could use it: **0.1** — convergence of the Taylor series at a point. **E2** — Monte Carlo running mean converging at 1/√N (same brace-and-decimal device but with noise). **C2** — binomial price converging to Black-Scholes as steps increase (oscillating convergence). **B1** — expected hitting time sums.
- Notes on execution: show the digits, not just the dot; the moment a digit freezes is the pedagogical beat. Tag: **build-up**.

### T4. Radius of convergence: a dot that converges inside, diverges outside
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter10.py` — `RadiusOfConvergenceForLnX` (`show_bounds`, `show_converging_point`, `show_diverging_point`), `BoundedRadiusOfConvergence`
- Topic illustrated there: Taylor series of ln x around 1 converging only on (0, 2]
- What moves / what the viewer does: a centre line at x=1 splits into two bound lines at 0 and 2; a dot on successive approximation graphs settles at x=1.5 then, moved to x=2.5, flies off as degree increases.
- What it makes visible that text cannot: convergence is a property of the *input point*, with a sharp boundary.
- Our chapters that could use it: **0.1** — essential. **D3** — Heston/SABR series expansions valid only near ATM (Hagan's formula breaks for extreme strikes); **C2** — stability region of explicit finite differences in **F1** (a step size inside/outside the stable region).
- Notes on execution: run the same term-by-term loop at two input points side by side. Tag: **comparison** / **zoom/continuum-limit**.

### T5. Second term of the Taylor series as a triangle of area under a graph
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter10.py` — `SecondTermIntuition` (`introduce_area`, `nudge_end_point`, `point_out_triangle`, `compute_triangle_area`)
- Topic illustrated there: why the ½f″ dx² term appears — area under a sloped line over a nudge dx is rectangle + triangle
- What moves / what the viewer does: the end point of an area region slides by dx; the sliver is decomposed into a rectangle (f dx) and a small triangle (½ f′ dx²).
- What it makes visible that text cannot: the dx² term has a geometric home; it is not a formality.
- Our chapters that could use it: **B4** — Itô: the ½σ²S² term is the triangle that does not vanish because (dW)² = dt. **D1** — gamma P&L as the triangle under the delta line. **C4** — gamma as the curvature correction to delta hedging.
- Notes on execution: colour the rectangle and triangle differently; show both shrinking as dx→0 but the triangle shrinking *faster*, then in the Itô version show the triangle *not* shrinking faster. Tag: **geometric-reinterpretation**.

### T6. Secant line sliding into a tangent line (dx → 0)
- Source: `once_useful_constructs/graph_scene.py` — `get_secant_slope_group`, `animate_secant_slope_group_change`; `_2017/eoc/chapter2.py` — `SecantLineToTangentLine`, `TCubedExample`, `DsOverDtGraphically`; `_2017/eoc/chapter5.py` — `GraphOfTwoToT`
- Topic illustrated there: derivative as the limit of rise/run; d(2^t)/dt
- What moves / what the viewer does: a dx-line and df-line form a right triangle on the graph with labels; `animate_secant_slope_group_change` interpolates dx toward 0 while the secant rotates into the tangent; the ratio can be printed as a changing decimal.
- What it makes visible that text cannot: the ratio stabilising before dx reaches 0 — the limit is "approach", not "plug in zero".
- Our chapters that could use it: **C4** — delta as the slope of price vs spot; finite-difference delta with bump size as the knob (also **F2** — bump-and-revalue vs AD). **B3** — the *failure* of this picture for Brownian paths (secant slopes do not converge; links to quadratic variation). **F1** — finite difference stencils.
- Notes on execution: a dx slider that also prints the finite-difference ratio; for the Brownian contrast, run the same widget on a sampled path and let the ratio blow up. Tag: **zoom/continuum-limit**.

### T7. Zoom into a curve until it looks straight (and into a nonlinear map until it looks linear)
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter2.py` — `TinyMovement` (ZoomedScene); `_2017/eoc/chapter3.py` — `DerivativeFromZoomingInOnSine`; `_2017/eoc/chapter7.py` — `EpsilonDeltaExample`, `LHopitalExample` (`zoom_in_to_trouble_point`); `_2015/ka_playgrounds/jacobian_animations.py` — `ExampleMultivariableFunctionWithZoom`, `JacobianDeterminantAnimation`
- Topic illustrated there: local linearity; ε-δ; L'Hôpital; Jacobian as the local linear map
- What moves / what the viewer does: a small rectangle on the main picture is magnified in a picture-in-picture; the curve inside becomes a line; in the Jacobian version a dense local grid is drawn, the plane is warped nonlinearly, and the inset shows the local grid as a parallelogram.
- What it makes visible that text cannot: "smooth means locally linear" as a literal visual experience; and the scale at which that stops being true.
- Our chapters that could use it: **B3** — the contrast: zoom into a Brownian path and it *never* becomes straight (self-similarity), the key picture for quadratic variation. **B4** — Itô's lemma as local linearisation that must keep the second-order term. **A2** — Cholesky/PCA as linear maps on a Gaussian cloud (zoom of the Jacobian idea). **D3** — local vol as a locally-Black-Scholes model.
- Notes on execution: for Brownian motion the zoom must re-sample the path at finer resolution (Lévy construction) so the roughness persists. Tag: **zoom/continuum-limit**.

### T8. Riemann rectangles refined to an area; rings unwrapped into rectangles
- Source: `once_useful_constructs/graph_scene.py` — `get_riemann_rectangles`, `get_riemann_rectangles_list`, `transform_between_riemann_rects`; `_2017/eoc/chapter1.py` — `ApproximateOneRing` (`slice_into_rings`, `straighten_ring_out`), `GraphRectangles` (`unwrap_rings_onto_graph`, `let_dr_approah_zero`); `_2017/eoc/chapter8.py` — `PiecewiseConstantPlot`, `FundamentalTheorem`; `_2017/eoc/chapter9.py` — `FiniteSample`, `FiniteSampleWithMoreSamplePoints`
- Topic illustrated there: area of a circle, integral as limit of sums, average of a continuous function
- What moves / what the viewer does: a circle is sliced into concentric rings; one ring is pulled out and straightened into a near-rectangle of width dr and height 2πr; all rings stand up side by side on a graph of 2πr; the rectangles are then halved in width repeatedly (`transform_between_riemann_rects`, with colour gradient preserved) until they fill the triangle. For averages, vertical sample lines are copied into a numerator "line + line + line…" over "(num. samples)".
- What it makes visible that text cannot: the integral as a *re-arrangement* of the same stuff; the refinement limit as a continuous process; the average as a sum of heights divided by count that becomes an integral divided by length.
- Our chapters that could use it: **0.2** — binomial bars refining into the normal density (de Moivre–Laplace); **A1** — expectation as area; **C3** — the risk-neutral price as ∫ payoff × density, shown as rectangles under payoff×pdf; **E1** — ES as the average of the tail: sample lines in the tail averaged. **C2** — the binomial tree's terminal distribution as bars morphing into a curve.
- Notes on execution: keep rectangle colours as a gradient so the eye tracks the same x across refinements; the "unwrap" morph needs the ring to be parametrised so Transform maps inner edge to bottom. Tag: **zoom/continuum-limit** and **transformation/morph**.

### T10. Nudge a geometric quantity and watch the change decompose (d(x²), product rule as area)
- Source: `_2017/eoc/chapter3.py` — `NudgeSideLengthOfSquare` (`increase_area`, `examine_thin_rectangles`, `show_smaller_dx`), `NudgeSideLengthOfCube`, `OneOverX`, `SquareRootOfX`; `_2017/eoc/chapter4.py` — `IntroduceProductAsArea` (`nudge_x`, `write_df`, `show_thinner_dx`), `ThreeLinesChainRule`, `SumRule`

## 3. Top 10 ideas for our chapters

1. **0.1 Taylor** — T1 + T2 + T4 together: three coefficient knobs the learner uses to fit cos x (discovery), then the automatic term-by-term build-up hugging the curve, then the same loop at a point outside the radius of convergence for ln x. The code is a direct template (`ExampleApproximation`, `ConstructQuadraticApproximation`, `RadiusOfConvergenceForLnX`).
2. **B4 Itô's lemma** — T10 (nudged square) with a random ±√dt step: the "corner square" dx² that vanishes in ordinary calculus but has expected area dt for Brownian steps. Pair with T45 (second difference) and T5 (triangle under the slope line) for the ½σ²S² term.
3. **B3 Brownian motion / quadratic variation** — T7 inverted: zoom into a sampled path and it never straightens; T26 log–log plot of summed squared increments vs step size (slope reveals the order of variation); T34 Lévy-construction refinement with a tracked time point.
4. **A2 Cholesky and PCA** — T13 applied to a dot cloud (independent Gaussians sheared into a correlated cloud, faded original kept), T16 eigenvectors as the directions that only stretch, T18 projection onto a rotatable axis with the projected spread read off (that *is* PCA), T14 determinant collapse for collinearity (also A3 multicollinearity).
5. **C2 Binomial → Black–Scholes** — T28 Pascal's triangle synchronised with the tree, T8 bars refining into the normal density, T33 layer-doubling morph of the tree, T3 running decimal of the price converging (with its characteristic oscillation).
6. **D1 Delta hedging P&L** — T11 two "cars": continuously hedged vs discretely hedged portfolio on a shared clock with hedge frequency as the knob; T10 strips for delta/gamma/theta attribution; T39 ledger rows accruing per rebalance.
7. **E2 Monte Carlo** — T24 resample/condition/re-describe the procedure (variance reduction as changing the *text* of the sampling procedure while the outcome distribution stays), T27 dots-in-a-circle opener, T3 running mean with brace, T44 error band shrinking with N.
8. **B5 Change of measure** — T22 sample-space rectangles with the dots fixed and the cells re-stretched (same outcomes, different areas) as the intuitive Radon–Nikodym picture; T30 slide-vs-stretch for drift change under exp.
9. **C1 / H2 Payoff decomposition** — T19 pointwise stacking of payoff graphs and T37 dissection: vanilla payoff pieces that slide and snap to assemble put–call parity, risk reversals, autocallables, accumulators; T15 weight knobs with a swept trail for strips.
10. **E3 Portfolio theory** — T15 two knobs sweeping the reachable (μ, σ) set into the frontier; T25 N weight sliders under a budget constraint that co-move; T47 cut-points-on-an-interval for long-only weights; T12 descent on the risk surface.

Honourable mentions: T23 (photons through filters) for Poisson arrivals and barrier knock-outs (**B1**, **H1**); T40 reversed-edge flashing for reverse-mode AD in **F2**; T38 (whole-derivation re-flow) as the architectural pattern for every slider chapter; T42 curves drawn left-to-right for walk-forward overfitting in **G3**; T46 "step must stay on the surface" for the Black–Scholes PDE in **C4**.
