## 1. What a fresher must walk away feeling

Anything can be made to fit the past; the only test of a model is a stretch of data it never saw — and most of what is called machine learning is a discipline for not cheating on that test.

## 2. Why a fresher should care (three hook candidates)

**Hook A — the puzzle: predict the NIFTY with a curve.** Take the last sixty closes of the real record (10 July to 5 October 2026) and draw the curve that best fits them. A straight line misses the dots by 365 points on average; a 5th-degree curve by 157; a 15th-degree curve by 72 — it practically passes through them. Now read each curve twenty trading days ahead, to 3 November, when the index actually closed at 23,811. The line says 22,641. The 5th-degree curve says 7,448. The 15th-degree curve says 179 million. Nothing in the fitting told you which to trust; the fit got *better* as the forecast got worse. Strongest hook: real data, a number the reader knows (D1's 23,811), and a failure of absurd size that the reader caused with a dial.

**Hook B — the story with stakes: the model that beat nothing.** Tsay's own textbook trial (Example 4.7): a neural network with three inputs and two hidden nodes, trained on 864 months of IBM returns, fitted the training months better than a one-lag regression (residual error 6.56 against 6.61). On the 24 months it had never seen, its squared forecast error was 91.74; the one-lag regression's was 91.70; *predicting the training-sample average every month* scored 91.85. Twenty-odd parameters bought nothing, and Tsay adds that re-running the fit gives anywhere from 89.46 to 93.65. Good as Figure 3's caption: a published author showing the reader what honest out-of-sample numbers look like.

**Hook C — the everyday object: a tailor's dummy.** A suit cut to fit the dummy exactly — every bump — fits nobody else; a suit cut to the dummy's general shape fits most people. Too loose fits nobody well either. The three fits are under-fitting, the right fit, and over-fitting, and the only way to know which you have is to try it on a person the tailor never measured. Standfirst material.

**Recommendation: A opens (Figure 0), C is the standfirst's image, B closes Figure 3.**

### Common misconceptions

1. **"A better fit is a better model."** Figure 0's whole point: the in-sample error falls monotonically with flexibility; the out-of-sample error does not.
2. **"More data in the model (more lags, more features) can only help."** On the real record, 60 lags of yesterday's move sizes predict tomorrow's *worse than guessing the average* (test error 0.3657 vs 0.3666); four lags do best (8.2 % better than guessing).
3. **"Shrinking coefficients toward zero throws information away."** Ridge on all 60 lags at λ ≈ 1,800 (0.3384) comes within half a percentage point of the best hand-picked four lags (0.3364), and it turns 25 negative coefficients (yesterday's big move making tomorrow's smaller — nonsense) into a smooth decaying profile.
4. **"Cross-validation finds the true model."** It finds a *good enough* setting without touching the test set: here it picks λ ≈ 560 (test error 0.3446) where the test set would have picked ≈ 1,800 (0.3384); both beat the unregularised fit's 0.3657 by a wide margin. And one fold (May 2019–Dec 2020, with March 2020 inside) carries most of the CV error.
5. **"Shuffle the days before splitting."** Days next to each other share their volatility; shuffled folds leak tomorrow into today's training set. G1 uses contiguous blocks and says why in one sentence; G3 does leakage properly.
6. **"The test set can be looked at once more."** Every look turns it into a training set. The chapter's test set (31 Jan 2024–3 Nov 2026, 683 days) is touched exactly once per figure, and the page says so.

## 4. Three illustration concepts, in three DIFFERENT modes

### Concept 1 — "Fit the index" (break-it dial with a bet, on the real closes)
The last 60 real closes before 5 October 2026 as dots; dial **degree** 1 → 15; the fitted curve drawn through the dots and extended 20 trading days to 3 November; the 20 real closes that followed revealed on toggle. Readouts: how far the curve misses the dots it was fitted to (in-sample rmse: 365 → 72 points), how far it misses the 20 days it never saw (461 → 53 million), the curve's reading for 3 November against the real 23,811. Bet first: "which degree will be closest on 3 November — 1, 5 or 15?" Makes visible: the fit improving as the forecast collapses. One dial, one toggle, three readouts.

### Concept 2 — "Tomorrow's move from yesterday's" (two-curve explorable with a dial and a segmented)
Predict the size of tomorrow's NIFTY move (|return|, in %) from the sizes of the last k days' moves by least squares (A3 with k columns). Dial **lags** k 1 → 60. Two curves against k: the error on the training days (1 Apr 2016–30 Jan 2024, 1,940 days) and on the test days (31 Jan 2024–3 Nov 2026, 683 days), each shown as a percentage of the error of just guessing the average (so the two periods are comparable: the training years hold March 2020). Training error falls for ever; test error bottoms at 4 lags (8.2 % better than guessing) and at 60 lags is back to guessing (0.2 % better). Segmented **training set**: all 1,940 days / the last 250 — on 250 days, 60 lags are 29 % *worse* than guessing. A horizontal line: A5's EWMA (3.3 % better). Makes visible: the U, and that it gets sharper with less data. Two controls, three readouts (training error, test error, best k so far).

### Concept 3 — "The leash" + "five folds" (coefficient bars with a dial; then a step-through)
(a) Keep all 60 lags; dial **λ** (the leash) 0 → 100,000 on a log scale; the 60 coefficients drawn as bars; the test error beside. At 0: 25 bars negative, test worse than guessing. At 1,000–3,000: a smooth decaying profile, test 7.2–7.7 % better — within half a point of the best k, with no choosing. At 100,000: all bars near zero, back to guessing. Readouts: test error, negative coefficients, the sum of the bars. (b) The training set cut into five dated blocks; step through: fit on four, score the fifth; the five scores (the 2019–20 block scores 1.39 against 0.21–0.53 for the others); the CV curve against λ with its minimum at ≈ 560; then — once — the test curve, minimum at ≈ 1,800; both beat λ = 0. Tsay's Example 4.7 numbers as the caption's still. Makes visible: choosing a setting without peeking, and what the chosen setting is worth on the day.
