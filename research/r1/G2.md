## 1. What a fresher must walk away feeling

With no label there is nothing to predict and nothing to score — so unsupervised learning is the art of choosing what to keep: which days belong together, which directions carry the variation, how few numbers describe a window of history — and every answer depends on a choice you made first (the features, their units, k), which the data cannot make for you.

## 2. Why a fresher should care (three hook candidates)

**Hook A — the puzzle: sort the days.** Every trading day since February 2016 as a dot: how much the index moved that day, and how rough the previous twenty days had been. Ask a machine to sort 2,663 dots into two piles with no instructions beyond "make the piles tight". It puts 36 days in one pile and 2,627 in the other — and the 36 are 13 March to 8 May 2020, nothing else. Nobody told it about a pandemic. Ask for three piles and it splits the calm pile into calm and choppy. Change the units of the axes, and the two piles become "down days" and "up days" instead. Strong: real, the reader's own history, and the second half is the lesson (units are a choice the algorithm cannot see past).

**Hook B — the story with stakes: level, slope, curvature, again.** Chapter E3 found that a yield curve's daily wobble is three shapes — level, slope, curvature. Take instead the last twenty days of move sizes, a 20-number vector for each of 2,663 days, and ask which directions carry the variation. The first is flat (all twenty days up together: the level of volatility, 28 % of the variance); the second rises from old to new (volatility rising or falling: 7 %); the third bends. The same three shapes, on a different object, found by the same arithmetic. The window with the largest "slope" score ends 20 April 2020 (volatility collapsing); the most negative ends 23 March 2020 (volatility exploding). Good as Figure 2's opening.

**Hook C — the everyday object: the packing list.** You cannot take the whole wardrobe; you pack the few things that cover most occasions, and you judge the packing by how little you miss. PCA packs by variance; an autoencoder packs by reconstruction error; k-means packs days into a few representatives. Standfirst material.

- **Hinton & Salakhutdinov, "Reducing the dimensionality of data with neural networks" (Science 2006)**: the autoencoder as "a nonlinear generalization of PCA"; and the honest finding here that on twenty days of move sizes the nonlinear version does *not* beat PCA (0.394 against the PCA floor 0.370 after 3,000 steps) — nonlinearity has to have something to find.

### Common misconceptions

1. **"The algorithm found the clusters."** It found the tightest piles *for the features and units you gave it*. Raw units: the volatility axis dominates and the piles are calm / covid. Standardised: down days / up days / covid. Same days, same algorithm.
2. **"There is a right k."** There is no label to score, so no test set; the elbow is a judgment and the silhouette a heuristic. Here they disagree (silhouette says 2; the elbow is somewhere in 3–5).
3. **"PCA needs lots of assets."** Any vector works: twenty consecutive move sizes make a 20-vector, and PCA finds level, slope and curvature exactly as E3 found them on yields.
4. **"The first component explains most of the variance."** On this object it explains 28 % (Tsay's five stocks: 49 %); the first ten explain 69 %. Variation in move sizes is mostly idiosyncratic day to day.
5. **"An autoencoder is a smarter PCA."** A linear one with two hidden units converges to exactly PCA's two-component error (0.3697) — it *is* PCA, learned slowly by gradient descent. A tanh one on this data is worse after the same steps. Nonlinearity pays only when the data has a bend to find.
6. **"Unsupervised means no choices."** Features, units, distance, k, the window length, the number of components — each is a choice; the algorithm makes none of them.

## 4. Three illustration concepts, in three DIFFERENT modes

### Concept 1 — "Sort the days" (step-through with a bet, then a units toggle)
2,663 real days as dots: x = that day's move (%), y = the previous 20 days' realised volatility (% a year). Bet first: "ask for two piles — how many days land in the smaller one? about 1,300 / about 300 / about 40". Step through Lloyd's algorithm: two random centres, every dot joins its nearest, each centre moves to the middle of its pile, repeat; the piles stop changing after 21 rounds; the small pile is 36 days, 13 March–8 May 2020. Segmented k 2 / 3 / 4 (sizes 2,627/36; 2,081/548/34; 1,474/886/272/31). Toggle **standardise the axes**: at k = 3 the piles become 1,057 down days / 1,574 up days / 32 covid days. Readouts: round, pile sizes, within-pile spread.

### Concept 2 — "How many piles?" (two-curve explorable)
Dial k 1 → 7: the within-pile spread (the elbow: 80,130 → 34,546 → 22,601 → 16,569 → …) and the silhouette (0.888, 0.625, 0.529, 0.503, 0.501, 0.455). Segmented **starting centres** (seed 1 / 2 / 3): at every k the three land on the same spread (22,601 at k = 4) by different numbers of rounds (30 / 26 / 29) — the warning about local optima stated and tested; on this cloud it does not bite. Readouts: spread, silhouette, rounds to converge. The caption says the honest thing: no label, no test set; two curves, two answers.

### Concept 3 — "Twenty days in two numbers" (loadings as shapes with a dial) + "a network that learns PCA" (step-through)
(a) Each day's last twenty move sizes as a 20-vector; PCA of the 2,663 vectors. The first three loadings drawn as bars over "20 days ago … yesterday": flat (level, 27.9 %), rising (slope, 7.3 %), bent (curvature, 4.8 %). Dial **components kept** 1 → 20: a chosen window (segmented: a calm fortnight / the window ending 23 March 2020) redrawn from k components over its dots; readouts variance explained (27.9, 35.2, 40.0 … 100 %), reconstruction error (0.411, 0.370, 0.342, 0.292 at 5, 0.175 at 10, 0 at 20), components. (b) A 20 → 2 → 20 network trained by gradient descent to reproduce its input: steps at 0, 10, 100, 1,000, 3,000 rounds; the loss curve falling to the PCA-2 floor 0.3697 for the linear network and only to 0.394 for the tanh one; the two hidden numbers of the March 2020 window plotted against its PC scores. Readouts: round, error, PCA floor.

## 6. Mathematics the chapter must get right, with reference numbers

- **Features.** Day t (t = 20 … 2,682 of the return record, 2,663 days, 2 Feb 2016–3 Nov 2026): x = 100·r_t; y = 100·√(252·mean(r²_{t−20..t−1})). Standardised: each axis less its mean over its sd.
- **k-means (Lloyd).** Centres start at k distinct random days (mulberry32 seed, floor(u·n) with rejection of repeats, as E2's RNG port); assign by squared Euclidean distance; recentre; stop when no centre moves more than 1e-9 or after 200 rounds. Raw, seed 1: k 2 → 21 rounds, within-pile spread 80,130, piles 2,627 / 36 (small pile 13 Mar–8 May 2020, centre (−0.10, 67.95)); k 3 → 20 rounds, 34,546, 2,081 / 548 / 34; k 4 → 30 rounds, 22,601, 1,474 / 886 / 272 / 31; seeds 1–3 reach the same spread at every k. Standardised, seed 1: k 2 → 2,189 / 474; k 3 → 1,574 (mean move +0.58 %) / 1,057 (−0.78 %) / 32 (vol 70.1 %); k 4 → 1,559 / 586 / 487 / 31.
- **Silhouette** s_i = (b_i − a_i)/max(a_i, b_i), mean over days; raw, seeds 1–3 agree: k 1 spread 186,662; k 2 0.888, 3 0.625, 4 0.529, 5 0.503, 6 0.501, 7 0.455.
- **PCA of 20-day windows** (X rows = a_{t−20..t−1}, oldest first; centred; covariance with 1/n). Eigenvalues' share: 27.9, 7.3, 4.8, 4.5, 4.3, 4.2 %; cumulative 27.9, 35.2, 40.0, 44.5, 48.8, 53.0; ten components 69.4 %. PC1 loadings 0.20 → 0.24 → 0.20 (all positive); PC2 +0.30 (oldest) → −0.30 (newest); PC3 −0.32, …, +0.29, …, −0.32. Reconstruction MSE with k components: 0.4113, 0.3697, 0.3424, 0.2921 (5), 0.1747 (10), 0 (20); total variance 0.5703. Extreme scores: PC1 max window ends 7 Apr 2020 (18.1); PC2 max 20 Apr 2020 (+7.3), min 23 Mar 2020 (−9.6, PC1 12.0); the calmest window (PC1 min) ends 23 Jun 2017. Sign convention: PC1 positive, PC2 positive on the oldest day.
- **Autoencoder** 20 → 2 → 20, full-batch gradient descent on mean squared reconstruction error, weights initialised N(0, 0.1²) from mulberry32 normals (seed 1; W1 then W2, row-major), biases 0. Linear, learning rate 0.2: loss 0.565 (0), 0.543 (10), 0.399 (100), 0.372 (1,000), 0.3697 (3,000) = PCA-2 floor. tanh, learning rate 0.5: 0.566, 0.549, 0.464, 0.402, 0.394. (The TS engine reproduces the linear run to 1e-8 and the tanh run to 1e-6 — Math.tanh and numpy's differ in the last bits.)
- **Tsay's still (Example 9.3):** λ̂₁ = 2.456 of 5 (49.1 %), e₁ = (0.342, 0.474, 0.387, 0.503, 0.505); λ̂₂ = 1.145 (22.9 %).
- **Tests.** Pile sizes and rounds equal the key for k 2/3/4 (raw and standardised); within-pile spread monotone in k; silhouette at k 2 above 0.85; seeds 1–3 agree at every k; eigen-shares, loadings (to sign), reconstruction MSEs, extreme-score dates; AE losses at the logged steps; the linear AE's final loss within 1e-4 of the PCA-2 floor; tanh's above it.
