# D1 Delta hedging and P&L attribution: gamma vs theta, implied vs realised, the discrete-hedging leak

_Phase R1 deep research note (sprint of 2026-10-22/23). Options for discussion with the sponsor, not decisions. Prerequisites assumed: C3 (the formula; implied vol), C4 (delta, gamma, theta and the PDE ½σ²S²Γ + Θ + rSΔ = rV). The catalogue names drwong's delta-hedging lab "the strongest tool on the site" and lists its gaps (empty narration, plug residual, unexplained MC button); this note keeps its panel pair and fixes the gaps, and adds the two things no site has: a story and a test number that is actually right._

## 1. What a fresher must walk away feeling

Selling an option and hedging it is a race between the cash you collect every day for the option losing time value and the cash you bleed every time the stock jumps and you must re-balance — the race is a dead heat only if the stock turns out to wobble exactly as much as the price you sold at assumed, and because you can only re-balance now and then, even a dead heat leaves you with a small, random, shrinking-with-frequency leftover.

## 2. Why a fresher should care (three hook candidates)

**Hook A — the story with stakes: the bank that sold a call for $300,000.** Hull's Chapter 14 opener (p. 299–300): a financial institution has sold, for $300,000, a 20-week call on 100,000 shares at $49 with strike $50; the Black–Scholes value is $240,000, "so the institution has sold the option for $60,000 more than its theoretical value. But it is faced with the problem of hedging the risks." Do nothing and "the cost … is liable to range from zero to over $1,000,000"; buy the shares and "if the stock price drops to $40, the financial institution loses $900,000". Then Table 14.2: twenty weekly rows of stock price, delta, shares bought or sold, cumulative cost — and the hedged cost comes out at $263,300 on one path and $256,600 on another, both "close to but not exactly the same as the Black–Scholes price of $240,000". The stakes are explicit, the table is a real artefact, and Hull's one-liner on *where the cost comes from* is the chapter's thesis: "The scheme generally involves selling stock just after the price has gone down and buying stock just after the price has gone up. It might be termed a buy-high, sell-low scheme!" (p. 307–308). **Strongest hook** because the numbers are real, the loss is visible and the moral is one sentence.

**Hook B — the puzzle: "two traders, same option, same path, one is calm and one is sweating."** Wilmott Ch. 12 (p. 200–203): hedge with the *actual* volatility and your final profit is locked (V(σ) − V(σ̃)) but your daily mark-to-market is noisy — "On a mark-to-market basis you could lose before you gain" (p. 202); hedge with the *implied* volatility and "the daily profit is deterministic, there aren't any dX terms … This is a far nicer way to make money" (p. 202) but the total is path-dependent. Figures 12.2 and 12.3 side by side: jagged lines converging to one number vs smooth monotone lines ending at different numbers. The puzzle ("which delta do you use?") is "one of those questions that people always ask, and one that no one seems to know the full answer to" (p. 199). Good hook for the mid-chapter turn; too subtle to open with.

**Hook C — the everyday object: Natenberg's roulette table and the "race".** Natenberg p. 83: the hedger is a roulette player who has bought a bet below its fair value: "On any one bet he will most likely lose, since there is only one way for him to win but 37 ways for him to lose … We can replicate the long-term probability by reassessing our position at regular intervals, and then making appropriate changes in the position so that each new interval represents a new bet." And p. 89: "We might think of the hedge as a race between the loss in time value of the June 100 call and the cash flow resulting from the adjustments, with the theoretical pricing model acting as the judge." Easy and memorable; it becomes the chapter's running metaphor rather than its opener.

**Strongest: A opens, C names the race, B is the twist in the last third.**

### Common misconceptions

- "Theta is my expected profit." Wilmott p. 772: "the commonly held belief that 'time decay is the expected profit and loss (P&L) from a position' is wrong. The expected P&L from a self-financed and delta-hedged option position is zero." Theta pays for gamma; the *expectation* is set by σ_imp vs σ_real only.
- "If I hedge, I lock in the edge." Only with the actual vol and continuous trading (Wilmott Case 1); otherwise the total is path-dependent — Natenberg's futures hedge made 62.81 on the model's 63.00 and the stock hedge 8,282 on 8,300, but Wilmott Figs 12.2/12.3 show the spread of outcomes.
- "Hedging more often always helps." Variance falls like 1/n (sd like 1/√n), costs rise like n; Hull p. 309 and Bouchouev's interior optimum.
- "The hedging error averages out so it doesn't matter." Wilmott p. 777: "Just because this averages out to zero does not mean it should be ignored"; the real distribution's factor 1.3–2.3 (Table 47.1).
- "Realised vol is the sample standard deviation." Moontower: subtracting the mean in a trending week dropped the number from 50.1 % to 20.4 %; the hedger's realised variance is the sum of squared moves, no mean.
- Engine-level: drwong's "theory marker" ½S²Γ₀(σ²imp − σ²real)T freezes gamma at t = 0 and overstates the mean (see §6: −3.66 vs simulated −3.06); the honest marker is Wilmott's V(σreal) − V(σimp) or a Monte Carlo mean.

### Concept 1 — "Hedge the book for 30 days" (game, then the 1,000-path histogram)

3. 5. The reveal line: the theta bars sum to roughly ½σ²_imp S²Γ·T, the gamma bars to roughly ½ S²Γ·(realised variance); their difference is the P&L; the formula ½ΓS²[(ΔS/S)² − σ²_imp Δt] per day appears *under* the bars it explains, never before.
6. "Run 1,000 of these." The histogram of final P&L with the zero line, the sample mean, and two markers: Wilmott's V(σreal) − V(σimp) and the drwong first-order marker, labelled "exact expectation" and "rule of thumb". 7. The frequency control: expiry-only / weekly / daily / 4×daily. The histogram narrows; a readout "sd ÷ premium = 0.43 → 0.19 → 0.09" reproduces Hull Table 14.4; a small inset plots sd vs n on log axes with the √(1/n) line (B14/B55 "the leak shrinking like √Δt").

**Makes visible:** that gamma losses arrive in lumps and theta in a trickle (text can say it; only the bars show the *rhythm*), that the mean is set by the vol gap and the spread by the frequency, and that the two are independent (freeport's "hedging error is independent of average trade profitability"). **Cost:** high — the engine (path, BS, ledger, attribution, MC, histogram), but every piece is already in drwong's lab, so the form is proven. **Risk:** five bars plus a ledger is a lot on one screen for a fresher; the narration line and the "one row at a time" pacing are the mitigation; the formula must stay beneath the bars.

## 6. Mathematics the chapter must get right, with reference numbers

**Identities (BS, continuous yield q, hedging a short option with Δ shares and a cash account):**
- One-step P&L of the hedged book, hedging with the implied vol σ̃: ΔΠ ≈ −ΘΔt − ½Γ(ΔS)² + carry − costs, with Θ, Γ at σ̃ (drwong's signs for a *short* option; Natenberg's and Moontower's for a long one are the mirror).
- With the PDE ½σ̃²S²Γ + Θ + (r − q)SΔ = rV: ΔΠ ≈ ½ΓS²[σ̃²Δt − (ΔS/S)²] per step for the short hedger (quantvault); summed, Wilmott (12.1): dΠ = ½(σ̃² − σ²)S²Γⁱ dt for the seller, total PV ½(σ̃² − σ²)∫₀ᵀ e^{−r(t−t₀)}S²Γⁱ dt (Wilmott p. 202; Gatheral (3.4) p. 28 is the expectation of the same).
- Hedging with the actual vol: locked profit V(S,t;σ) − V(S,t;σ̃), noisy marks (Wilmott p. 200–201); at any σ_h, eq. (12.2).
- Discrete hedging error per step ½(φ² − 1)σ²S²Γ δt with φ ~ N(0,1): mean 0, chi-squared(1) shape, 68 % below the mean (Wilmott p. 769–770); total sd O(√δt), i.e. ∝ 1/√n for n rehedges (Wilmott p. 770; Zhang 1999 via ar5iv 1004.4526; Grannan–Swindle via Lyuu).
- Rule of thumb: sd(P&L) ≈ √(π/4) · vega · σ / √n (Kamal–Derman 1999; freeport citing *Trading Volatility* p. 95). Reason: n rehedges = an n-sample estimate of σ², whose error is ≈ σ√(π/4)/√n in σ units, times vega.
- Hull's performance measure = sd(discounted hedging cost)/BS price (p. 302, 308).
- Realised variance for the hedger = Σ(ΔS/S)² (no mean subtracted; Moontower).
