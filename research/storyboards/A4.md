**Working title:** *Does it come home?*
**Standfirst:** Some series have a home they keep coming back to. Others wander off and never return. A trader's whole edge can be the difference — and no chart can tell you which you are looking at. This page is a game about telling them apart, and then the one test that can.
**Assumes:** you can read a line chart and know what an average is. That is all.
**Characters:** none. The series are the characters.

## The game, in rounds (each round is one figure)

| Round | What the reader does | New rule learned | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Nine charts** | Ticks which of nine series "keep their rules over time" (a price, its daily changes, a volatility index, a spread, a seasonal volume, an aperiodic cycle, a trend, an FX rate, a yield — mostly simulated, labelled; the two real ones are NIFTY and its daily changes). Submits; each panel flips with one reason. Score out of 9. | What "stationary" means — and that almost everyone swaps the two that matter: the price looks like it has a level (it doesn't); the wild-looking cycle is stationary (it is) | guess-then-reveal gallery |
| **2 · The forgetting machine** | A marble in a bowl, kicked by news every tick; **one dial: the bowl's steepness φ**. Turn it: 0.5 snaps home, 0.9 wanders but returns, 1.0 is a flat table (a random walk), 1.05 flies off. Press "release 500 marbles": the cloud settles into a shape — or keeps widening forever at φ = 1 | today = φ × yesterday + news. The bowl is a picture of that multiplication, not gravity (said on the figure). A home exists only if φ < 1; the "home" is where the marbles pile up | physical simulation, one dial |
| **3 · How fast does it forget?** | Presses "+1 day" on a single kick and watches it shrink by φ each click; the shrinking bars are the autocorrelation. Then: the same bars for the real NIFTY level (stay near 1 for 30 days) vs its daily changes (vanish at lag 1) | The ACF is the honest version of the eye: slow decay = no home. Differencing is the one-step fix | step-through (+1 day), then two real ACF panels |
| **4 · The pairs trade** | Round with stakes: a spread between two bank stocks that has crossed zero forty times. "It always comes back — buy when it's low." The reader picks: rubber band or drunk? Then the **Dickey–Fuller idea** in one picture: regress today's change on yesterday's level; is the slope zero (drunk) or negative (band)? The reader drags the line; the test's own number appears; one footnote says the critical values are special | A finite expected return time is the trade's whole edge; forty crossings can be luck. The DF test is one regression and one slope | artefact-style (the spread chart) + one drag |
| **5 · Break it: fool the eye** | Ten fresh series, five random walks and five mean-reverting (φ = 0.95–0.98), unlabelled; guess by eye, then press "test". Score out of 10, with the honest sentence "by eye you got k; the test got all ten" | No chart can tell them apart near φ = 1; only the test can — and even the test needs a long sample | game, final round |
