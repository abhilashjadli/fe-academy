**Working title:** *Three stories, one curve*
**Standfirst:** Black–Scholes draws a flat line through the smile, so something in it has to give, and the thing that gives is the one number it never let move: the wobble. Let the wobble depend on where the price is, and you get a smile. Let it have a random life of its own, and you get a smile. Let the price jump now and then, and you get a smile. All three draw the same curve through today's quotes to a tenth of a point — and they disagree completely about tomorrow. This page is about the three stories, the one knob in each that makes the tilt, and the question that tells them apart.
**Assumes:** C3's cloud and dial, D2's smile (17.0 % at 22,250 sloping to 15.5 % at 23,050) and the idea of a weight on futures from B5. Each is restated where used.
**Characters:** none.

## Close (short)
**So what is "beyond Black–Scholes"?** — three ways to let the wobble move: by place (local volatility: a map, exact for today's prices by construction, wrong about how the smile moves), by its own random life (Heston: five knobs, two of which — tilt and curvature — the chain can read, three of which are promises), by jumps (Merton: a bell plus rare bad days, a smile that dies out with time, and a risk no hedge can remove). SABR is the ruler desks use to draw a smile through a few quotes. The four failure stills, one sentence each: local vol's forward smiles come out flat; Heston's short-dated skew is too weak; SABR's wings can price a negative probability; Merton's jumps cannot be hedged away. Gatheral's verdict as the footer: the model that survives a desk is usually stochastic volatility *with* jumps. One formula at most, collapsed with the knob table: the three recipes side by side (dS/S = σ(S,t)dW; dS/S = √v dW₁, dv = κ(θ − v)dt + η√v dW₂, corr ρ; dS/S = σ dW + (J − 1)dN) and Dupire's rule in words. "Where it is used": every exotics desk prices barriers and autocallables (H1, H2) on one of these, and which one changes the price by percent, not basis points; the Monte Carlo of E2 is how Heston and Merton paths are actually run; D2's smile is the input these models are fitted to every morning. Last line, no formula; pull to E1 or H1 ("a product that pays only if the index never touches a level cares about the story, not the curve").

- The reader meets the knobs as rounds to lose before any symbol — and loses round 3 on purpose, which is the honest centre of the chapter (one expiry cannot see κ or θ).
- Each story is one figure, one dial, on the same cloud from C3, so "a smile is what you get when the wobble moves" is seen three times rather than said.
- The real chain is the artefact throughout; the surprise that three different stories fit it identically is the hook and the figure.
- Lightness: no formula outside the collapsed panel; SABR is a row, not a fourth story; the failure strip is four sentences.
