**Working title:** *Nothing to predict*
**Standfirst:** You cannot take the whole wardrobe. You pack the few things that cover most occasions and judge the packing by how little you miss. That is unsupervised learning: no label, nothing to predict, nothing to score against — only the question of what to keep. This page sorts ten years of NIFTY days into piles with no instructions beyond "make the piles tight", and the machine finds March 2020 by itself; then it compresses twenty days of market history into two numbers and finds chapter E3's level, slope and curvature again; then a small network learns to do the same compression and arrives, slowly, exactly where PCA started. Every answer depends on a choice — the features, their units, how many piles — that the algorithm cannot make for you.
**Assumes:** realised volatility (A5), PCA and level/slope/curvature (E3), hold-out and the U (G1). Each restated where used.

## Close (short)
**So what is unsupervised learning?** — sort (k-means: assign, recentre, repeat; the piles are the tightest for the features and units you gave it), compress (PCA: keep the directions that carry the variance; the error is the eigenvalues you dropped), rebuild (an autoencoder: a network trained to reproduce its input through a bottleneck; linear, it is PCA; nonlinear, it can do more when the data has more). No label means no held-out score, so k, the window, the units and the components are judgments — the silhouette and the elbow are aids, not tests. One formula at most, under Figure 2: the reconstruction error with k components equals the sum of the dropped eigenvalues. Collapsed: Lloyd's steps; the silhouette formula; why a linear autoencoder spans the PCA subspace (Baldi–Hornik); the gap statistic; hierarchical clustering in one line. "Where it is used": the "regime" flag on a morning risk report is Figure 0's pile; PCA of a vol surface drives its risk; clustering a correlation matrix before allocating (hierarchical risk parity). Last line, no formula; pull to G3 ("and the moment you use the piles to trade, they become a prediction — and chapter G1's rules return, with a trap of their own").
