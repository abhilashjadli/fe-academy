## Close (short)
**So what is an option?** — a contract that gives one side a choice and the other an obligation, paid for up front; what it pays at the end is a bent line, and bent lines add. Because a call and a put at the same strike add to a straight line, their prices are chained: call − put = index − strike, up to interest. That is why half the screen can be rebuilt from the other half — and it holds only for options usable on expiry day alone (NIFTY's are). One formula at most: c − p = S − K e^{−rT}. Collapsed: Hull's bounds and arbitrage tables, early exercise (deferred to C2's American toggle), the dividend term, the note that American options obey only an inequality. The chapter's own last line, no formula; pull to C2 ("where 249.15 comes from — and why the answer needs no opinion about the index").
