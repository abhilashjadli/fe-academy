**Working title:** *The slope, the bend and the clock*
**Standfirst:** Raj sold 100 lots of the 22,600 call on Friday for ₹16.2 lakh. His risk screen shows him five numbers for it, and on Monday morning the index has moved 153 points. This page is about those five numbers: they are the slope, the bend and the sinking rate of one curved surface, read at the spot where Raj is standing — and the surface sinks exactly as fast as it bends, which is why nobody gets curvature for free.
**Assumes:** you have seen a price curve bend (C3's cloud gave you one; it is redrawn here) and you remember from 0.1 that a curve near a point is "value + slope × step + ½ bend × step²". That is all.
**Characters:** Raj (the seller from C3, now with a position). Nobody else.

## Close (short)
**So what are the Greeks?** — the price of an option is a curved surface over "where the index is" and "how long is left"; delta is the slope where you stand, gamma the bend, theta how fast the floor drops, vega how much the whole surface inflates when the wobble dial turns; and the surface drops exactly as fast as it bends, so a position that gains from movement must pay for it by the day. One equation at most, in words then symbols: Θ + ½σ²F²Γ = rV. Collapsed: the closed forms (Δ = N(d₁), Γ = N′(d₁)/(Fσ√T), Θ, vega, ρ, with the e^{−rT} of the forward form); units (per calendar day ÷365, per trading day ÷252, per vol point ÷100); Hull's bank (S = 49, 100,000 shares, $240,000, Tables 14.2–14.3 → $263,300 / $256,600, Table 14.4's 0.43 → 0.09); the three misconceptions (delta ≈ probability only roughly, theta is not a risk to hedge, puts have positive gamma too); the gamma-neutral and vega-neutral hedges as two lines. "Where it is used": the risk line is read every morning on every desk; the gamma–theta tradeoff is D1's whole P&L; the higher legs of the same surface are C5; the surface's wobble dial being different per strike is D2. Last line, no formula; pull to C5 ("every one of these five numbers has its own slope and bend").

## Why this shape
- The risk line first, unread, is the real-world moment from the note §7 ("why is our theta so big?") made into the opening bet; every figure after it lights one tile, so the page has a visible finish line without any progress system.
- The 3-D surface is built from C3's curve in front of the reader and is never shown ready-made; the synced slice and the three colour-locked readouts are the figure — the surface is the stage for them. Fallback stated.
- One real day instead of a 30-day game: the weekend's three days of theta is a better lesson than a seeded path, and D1 owns the long game.
- Lightness: no Greek is defined before its picture; the closed forms are collapsed; units are printed on every tile; Raj's numbers are rupees per lot, not abstract decimals.
