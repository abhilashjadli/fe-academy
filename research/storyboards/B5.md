**Working title:** *The odds are not the price*
**Standfirst:** Priya is sure the stock is going up. Raj sets odds for a living and never bets on anything. They are both shown the same stock and the same ticket, and they disagree about everything — except, it turns out, what the ticket is worth today. This page is their argument, and why Raj wins it without ever saying what he thinks will happen.
**Assumes:** you know what an average is and what interest is. That is all.
**Characters:** two, with faces — **Priya** (a belief: 70 % up) and **Raj** (no belief; a hedge). Their faces are the legend swatches on every figure: Priya's colour is "what she expects", Raj's is "what it costs".

## Close (short)
**So what is the risk-neutral measure?** — Raj's coin: the one set of weights on the futures under which every traded thing is a fair bet after interest, so that today's price is simply the discounted average payoff. It is not anyone's belief, and not an assumption that people stop minding risk; it is the odds a bookmaker who never bets is forced to post. One formula at most: price today = discounted average of the payoff, under Raj's weights. Collapsed: Z(up) = 0.9265, Z(down) = 1.1750 as the "exchange rate" between the two coins; W̃ = W + θt; Shreve's statement. The chapter's own last line, no formula; pull to B6 ("Raj's average is also the answer to an equation nobody has written yet").

## Why this shape
- The disagreement is the content, so the characters are load-bearing: every one of Priya's lines is a belief a fresher holds, and the next figure breaks it.
- The spine is a dial that does nothing — the one explorable in the library whose reward is a needle refusing to move; the range toggle beside it stops "so probabilities are irrelevant" from forming.
- Raj is right every time, including when he refuses to forecast: "price with Q, forecast with P" lands without a slogan.
- Girsanov is a picture plus one sentence; nothing on the page is a derivation.
