**Working title:** *The market does not believe in one number*
**Standfirst:** On 23 October the 22,250 call was priced as if the index would wobble at 17.0 %; the 23,050 call, same index, same day, same expiry, at 15.5 %. The index can only wobble one way over those eleven days. So who is wrong? This page is about why the question is wrong — the formula was never a belief about the index, it is a language for comparing prices — and what the curve of those numbers, read strike by strike, says about the future the market is actually paying to avoid.
**Assumes:** C3's dial (implied volatility is the one input the screen does not fix), C1's law (a call and a put at one strike are one bet plus a forward), and A1's picture (the real index has fat tails). Each is restated in one line where it is used.

## Close (short)
**So what is the smile?** — the market's prices, translated into the formula's one unobservable input, do not come out as one number: far-below strikes translate to a higher wobble because the real index falls hard more often than the bell says, and the market charges for it. Read as a curve, the smile is the market's own odds for expiry day; read across time, a surface. The formula survives not as a belief but as the language the odds are quoted in. One formula at most, under the tents: the probability of landing near K ≈ e^{rT} × (C(K−h) − 2C(K) + C(K+h)) / h². Collapsed: parity forces call and put vols equal (C3 Figure 5 did it); the three coordinates a smile can be plotted in (strike, ln(K/F), ln(K/F)/√T — Natenberg's "all moves are relative"); Hull's single-jump frown (Table 15.3, 30.95 → 41.48 → 32.50) as the counter-example; the sparse-chain warning (a chain only sees the mass between its strikes — about half of it here); sticky strike vs sticky delta in two sentences (does the smile stay put when the index moves, or move with it? — equities tend to the first, FX to the second, and the hedge delta differs: Derman 1999); the 1987 paragraph (crashophobia; −22.9 % in a day; the skew exists since). "Where it is used": every vol surface on every desk; D3 (models that produce a whole surface from three knobs); H2 (FX quotes the smile as a risk reversal and a strangle); E1 (the implied density as a scenario set). Last line, no formula; pull to D3 ("three knobs that draw the whole curve — and the day they stop fitting").
