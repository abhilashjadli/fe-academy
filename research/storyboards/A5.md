**Working title:** *How fast should you forget?*
**Standfirst:** Nobody packs an umbrella because of the yearly average: after a stormy day you expect more storms, and after a dry fortnight you stop. Markets are the same — big days come in spells. So "how risky is tomorrow" is really a question about how fast to forget yesterday, and this page is about choosing that speed.
**Assumes:** you can read a bar chart and know what an average and a square are. That is all.
**Characters:** none. The 2,679 real days are the characters.

## Close (short)
**So what is a volatility model?** — a rule for how fast to forget. Equal weights forget nothing for a year then everything at once; the bucket forgets a fixed fraction a day; the bucket with a drip forgets and drifts home. EWMA and GARCH(1,1) are named as the two buckets; the one formula is the three-pipe recursion already on screen. Collapsed: likelihood, the flat ridge, Student-t, leverage. The chapter's own last line; pull to E1 ("the number VaR multiplies is the one you just chose").
