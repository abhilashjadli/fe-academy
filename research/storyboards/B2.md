**Working title:** *The meter that will not move*
**Standfirst:** A fair coin, a chip a toss, and you may leave whenever you like. Surely a clever enough plan — quit when ahead, double after a loss, bet big on a streak — beats it? Thousands have been sure. This page lets you try every plan at a table with a croupier, and shows the one number none of them touches, and the one thing that does.
**Assumes:** you know what an average is and what a fair coin is. That is all.
**Characters:** the croupier (one drawn face; speaks only to state a rule). The player is you.

## Close (short)
**So what is a martingale?** — a game where, knowing everything up to now, your best guess for tomorrow's chips is today's; no exit rule and no bet sizing moves the average, only peeking does. One formula at most: expected chips tomorrow, given today = chips today. Then **the information diary**, illustrated text with one drawing: the three-toss tree (4, ×2 or ×½) and a diary of four pages — before any toss (eight paths possible), after one (four), after two (two), after three (one); each page writes its own forecast of the end, and no page is ever torn out. A stopping rule is one you can write on today's page; a backtest using next year's index members reads tomorrow's. "Filtration" is written once, under the drawing. Collapsed: the four-line proof, the five properties, the fine print. The chapter's own last line; pull to B5 ("which average — and who moves the odds so the meter reads the price").

## Why this shape
- The meter is the invention: a martingale is a statement about conditional averages, not a mechanism; the meter makes it a thing you watch fail to move under your own hand.
- One table, one new rule per figure: theory is only the sentence Figure 3 made true; the peek is the break *inside* the game.
- The diary is static on purpose: filtrations are not interactive, and one drawing beats sixteen sets.
