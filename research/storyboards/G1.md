**Working title:** *Anything fits the past*
**Standfirst:** A suit cut to a tailor's dummy bump for bump fits nobody else; one cut to the dummy's general shape fits most people; one cut loose fits nobody well. You cannot tell which you have by looking at the dummy — only by trying it on someone the tailor never measured. That is the whole of machine learning's discipline, and this page does it to the NIFTY. First you will fit the last sixty days of the index with a curve and watch the forecast go from 22,641 to 179 million as the fit gets better. Then the three habits that stop it: keep days the model never sees; put the coefficients on a leash; choose the leash on days you have already set aside. Everything is chapter A3's least squares with more columns.
**Assumes:** A3 (a line through dots, the squared misses), A5 (the size of a day's move clusters; EWMA), E1 (the 500-day fence was an out-of-sample test). Each restated where used.

## Close (short)
**So what is supervised learning?** — least squares with more columns, and three habits. Hold out: the only error that counts is on days the fit never saw. Regularise: a penalty on the size of the coefficients trades a little fit for a lot of stability; ridge is the closed-form one. Validate: choose the penalty, the lag count, the model, on folds of the training data, and touch the test set once. One formula at most, under Figure 2: minimise Σ(y − Xβ)² + λΣβ², solved by (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀy. Collapsed: the bias–variance split in words; why the folds are contiguous blocks (neighbouring days share their volatility — G3); the lasso's diamond and why it zeroes; the one-standard-error rule; double descent in one sentence. "Where it is used": every fitted thing on this site — D3's smiles, F2's valley, E1's EWMA λ — was a fit with these risks; a desk's first question about a backtest is "how many versions did you try?". Last line, no formula; pull to G2 ("and when there is no label to hold out — just prices moving together — the question becomes what to keep").

## Why this shape
- The reader's own index and own number (23,811 from D1) open the chapter; the failure is caused by the reader's dial, at absurd size, on real data.
- Figures 1–3 are one model (lags of move size) seen three ways: how many columns, how tight a leash, how to choose without peeking.
- Every error on the page is relative to "guess the average", so the lesson survives March 2020 sitting in the training years.
- The only textbook numbers (Tsay) are in a caption, labelled, and make the same point on a different market.
- Lightness: one formula; one dial per figure; the test set touched once per figure, said once.
