**Working title:** *Less wobble for the same return — and the three ways a curve moves*
**Standfirst:** Four assets, and one of them is plainly the worst: lower return than another at higher risk. Would you ever hold it? Markowitz's answer in 1952 was the one nobody expected — yes, if it zigs when the others zag — and the menu of best mixes turned out to be a single curve. The same matrix that draws that curve answers a second question about any cloud of correlated numbers: which directions does it actually move in? Ask it of ten government-bond yields moving every day and the answer is three — the curve shifts, it tilts, it bends — and almost nothing else. This page is both questions, asked of one object.
**Assumes:** A2 (the tilted cloud; correlation as a shear), 0.2 (mean and width), E1 (the linear model's σ_P; Hull's two-asset benefit). Each restated where used.
**Characters:** none.

## Close (short)
**So what did the matrix say?** — asked for the least-wobble mix at each return, it draws the frontier, and the frontier holds assets nobody would hold alone; asked for the directions it moves in, it names them by size, and for a yield curve three names cover nearly everything: level, slope, curvature. The frontier is objective given the data and the point on it is yours; and the data are the weak part — Wilmott: *"only the volatilities are at all reliable"* — so the frontier moves when the window moves (a still: the same five assets on two five-year windows). One formula at most, under Figure 0: σ_P² = Σ WᵢWⱼ ρᵢⱼ σᵢσⱼ. Collapsed: the Lagrange solution and Wilmott's spreadsheet point; the tangent portfolio and the capital market line; beta and the single-index model (CAPM) in Wilmott's two lines; eigen-decomposition and Tsay's Result 9.1; PCA on changes not levels; "PCA finds directions, not causes" (Pérignon et al.); Litterman–Scheinkman's 89.5 %. "Where it is used": every fund's risk budget; a rates desk's DV01 ladder beside "PC1/PC2/PC3 exposure" and the day a DV01-flat book loses on a twist; E1's bond-book VaR; E2's quasi-random paths built on the Brownian covariance's own axes; F's one-factor credit model (PCA with k = 1 by assumption); G's one-, two- and three-factor rate models. Last line, no formula; pull to F ("forty borrowers, one factor: how a credit book's losses pile up").
