## Close (short)
**So what is Feynman–Kac?** — the statement that "today's value is the average over all the futures" and "today's value obeys a rule for how it changes as you move the starting point" are the same sentence written twice. The tree you built in C2 already does both: each node is the discounted average of its children, and "average of neighbours, corrected for curvature" is what a diffusion equation says. One formula at most: value(today, here) = discounted average of payoff over futures that start here — and that function solves the backward rule. Collapsed: the three-step proof (find the martingale, take the differential, set the dt term to zero), the discounted version, and why the average must be under B5's weights — which is why interest, not the stock's drift, sits in the rule. The chapter's own last line, no formula; pull to C3 ("the smooth curve at six months has a closed form, and two different arguments reach it").

## Why this shape
- The comic installs the theorem's three ideas (weighted average, only the next step matters, backwards) with no finance and no calculus.
- The split screen makes the theorem an *equality on screen*: one box, two writers, a tick when they agree. The clip is a "watch" tab inside the figure, so it never duplicates what follows.
- The drag is the desk fact (grid instant, simulator per-point); the x² check is the one case a fresher can verify by hand, so the equality is believed, not asserted.
- No characters after the opener: the two panels are the characters.
