**Working title:** *The weather has a habit*
**Standfirst:** Look at four months of real weather, one square per day: dry, some rain, heavy. The pattern is obvious — rain comes in spells — and yet "tomorrow depends only on today" turns out to describe it almost perfectly. This page takes that strip apart, finds the small machine inside it, and uses the same machine to explain why a drunk drifts and why buses arrive in clumps.
**Assumes:** you can count and you know what a fraction is. That is all.
**Characters:** one — the person at the bus stop (Figure 5 only).

## Close (short)
**So what is a Markov chain?** — a strip plus a table of fractions, where the next square is drawn from the row you are standing in and nothing else. The random walk is the same machine with states on a line; the bus process is the same idea on a clock, whose only state is "no bus yet". One formula at most: tomorrow's law = today's law × the table. Collapsed: the fixed point, the forgetting speed, Shreve's scaled walk, the Poisson count. The chapter's own last line; pull to B2 ("the drunk's average position never moves — and that, not the memory, is what a price has").
