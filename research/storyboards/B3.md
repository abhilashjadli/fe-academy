**Working title:** *The dial that reads the clock*
**Standfirst:** A car drives a jittery road. On its dashboard: a speedometer, an odometer, and a strange third dial that adds up the squares of every little move. Now read the dashboard twice as often, and twice as often again. Which dial settles to a steady reading? Almost everyone picks the wrong one — and the right one is the instrument the rest of this library is built on.
**Assumes:** you know what a slope is and you have seen a jagged line chart. That is all.
**Characters:** Ada and Mel, Figure 4 only, as legend swatches on the two fans.

## Close (short)
**So what is Brownian motion?** — a path whose steps over a short interval are as big as the square root of the interval; so it has no slope anywhere, no finite length, and exactly one steady tally — the squares dial, which reads the clock. One formula at most: the sum of squared moves → the time elapsed. The three names attach to Figures 2, 4 and 5 in one sentence each; Mel's closed form is *stated* with "B4 derives it". Collapsed: the defining properties, Shreve's scaled coin walk, realised volatility, the leash's exact solution. The chapter's own last line; pull to B4 ("the squares dial is about to become a toll on every curved function").

## Numbers for the engine (note §6)
Quadratic variation [W,W](T) = T, Var[(ΔW)²] = 2(Δt)² (Shreve Thm 3.4.3, pp. 102–103); fixed-seed bridge-refined path: Σ(ΔW)² = 0.775, 0.959, 0.985, 1.003 at n = 16 … 65,536, test |Q − 1| < 4√(2/n); Σ|ΔW| = 2.76, 12.5, 50.8, 204 (Hull p. 219); secant ratio 4, 16, 64, 256; per-step ratio ~ χ²₁, P(< 1) = 0.683, median 0.455. Bridge midpoint = average of ends + N(0, Δt/4). Hull Ex. 11.1 √5 = 2.236, Ex. 11.2 N(70, 30) (p. 221). GBM S₀ = 100, μ = 10 %, σ = 40 %: 110.52 / 102.02 at t = 1, 271.83 / 122.14 at t = 10 (Shreve 4.4.28, p. 148); A1 tie 98.76. Realised variance (Shreve 3.4.16, p. 107) on the real series: sd 1.02 %/day ≈ 16.2 %/yr. OU θ = 4 %, σ = 1 %, κ = 2: long-run sd 0.500 %, half-life 0.3466 yr; stepped sd at tick 0.10/0.25/0.50/0.75 = 0.527/0.577/0.707/1.000 %, ∞ at 1.00 (Shreve Ex. 4.4.10, pp. 150–151; riskhub).
