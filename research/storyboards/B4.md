**Working title:** *The square that would not vanish*
**Standfirst (the question, not the topic):** In ordinary calculus a tiny step squared is nothing. Prices take tiny steps all day. Why does squaring theirs leave something behind — and why does it make every option in the world slightly cheaper than you'd guess?
**Assumes:** you can take a derivative and you've seen a price chart. That is all. (B3's "squared wobbles add up to the time elapsed" is restated in one line where needed.)
**Characters:** Ada (keeps the squares) and Mel (keeps first-order books) — faces from the style lab.

## Figure 2 in beats (the main figure)

1. Still frame: a square of side x = 3, exactly 3Blue1Brown's. Prompt: "Nudge the side by 0.01. The area grows by two thin strips and a tiny corner. The corner is 0.0001 — safe to ignore. (0.1 said so.)"
2. "Now the side doesn't nudge; it *jitters*. Flip the coin." One flip: heads → side grows by √Δt: two amber strips and a blue corner appear. Tails → side shrinks: the two strips are drawn blue-outlined (removed), the corner is still filled. Caption: "Heads or tails, the corner is added. It is a square: it cannot be negative."
3. "Flip twenty times." Two dials beside the square: *strips so far* (wanders, crosses zero), *corners so far* (climbs). Caption: "The strips are a fair game. The corners are a clock."
4. The Δt dial: turn it from 1/10 to 1/160. The flips get smaller and more frequent; the strips dial is as jumpy as before; the corners dial reads the same total. Caption: "Smaller steps, more of them: the corners add up to the same thing — the time that passed. Cross out the sticker."
5. The equation written with the shapes as its terms: Δ(x²) = [two strips] + [corner]; then with symbols: d(x²) = 2x dW + dt. Caption: "This is the whole lemma for one function. The chain rule got a passenger."
6. "Now you": a check panel — x_T² − 2Σx·dx equals Σ(dx)² exactly, every time, and ≈ T. One button: "run again".

Observe (≤ 60 words): "In 0.1 the corner vanished because a nudge squared is tiny. Here each corner is tiny too — but a coin lands √Δt away, so the corner is Δt, and there are 1/Δt of them. Tiny times many is one. Everything in this chapter is that sentence."

## Figure 3 in beats (the derivation, lightened)

1. 0.1's three tiles: a bar f′Δx, a square ½f″Δx², a sliver. "Halve Δx" ×3: the square shrinks four times faster. Sticker on.
2. Swap the nudge for a jitter: Δx = aΔt + bΔW. The tiles multiply out into Δt², Δt·ΔW, ΔW² — the ΔW tile carries a coin.
3. "Halve Δt" ×4, areas printed as *expected* areas: bar halves; Δt² quarters; Δt·ΔW shrinks by 2.8; **ΔW² halves — same as the bar.**
4. Stickers go on everything that shrank faster than the bar. Two things remain: the bar and the ΔW² tile, now labelled "= b²Δt on average".
5. The box: dt·dt = 0, dt·dW = 0, dW·dW = dt — each cell flies in from its tile. (A table *earned*, not announced.)
6. The lemma assembles, colour-locked to its tiles: df = (f_t + a f_x + ½ b² f_xx) dt + b f_x dW. Collapsed panel underneath: "the full working, if you want it".
