**Working title:** *All he did was change his mind*
**Standfirst:** A trader sells four calls, buys the index against them, and is flat. He trades nothing, the index does not move — and by lunchtime he is short. All he did was change his mind about the wobble. Every strange-named Greek on this page is that one story told twice over: nudge one thing, and watch a number you thought was fixed drift. Only two of them are worth carrying in your head, and you will meet both before any symbol.
**Assumes:** C4's three words (slope, bend, clock) and C3's dial (the wobble is the one input you choose). That is all.
**Characters:** none drawn. Raj's book from C4 appears by name on the desk grid.

## Close (short)
**So what are the higher Greeks?** — slopes of slopes: how the slope you hedged with drifts when the wobble moves (vanna), when the clock moves (charm), and how the wobble-sensitivity itself bends (volga); the rest are the same question asked of gamma. For a single at-the-money option they are the residual after delta, gamma, theta and vega — under half a cent a day in Moontower's year of real data; they matter for books, wings and the last days before expiry. One formula at most, the hedger's line: dΔ ≈ Γ dS + vanna dσ + charm dt (+ ½ speed dS²). Collapsed: the glossary (vanna = ∂Δ/∂σ = ∂vega/∂S; volga = ∂²V/∂σ²; charm = ∂Δ/∂t in calendar time, so an out-of-the-money call's charm is negative; speed, zomma, color, veta) with the closed forms and the sign rules (vanna: −sign(d₂); volga: sign(d₁d₂)); the two misconceptions (vanna is not "positive for calls, negative for puts" — same sign for both at one strike; volga is not always positive — a thin negative band near the money); Taleb's identity vega = σ τ S² Γ with the moral that gamma and vega are one risk counted twice; the sticky-delta correction to delta in one line (D2). "Where it is used": the Monday-morning risk report whose deltas differ from Friday's with nothing traded ("did you look at your charm?"); FX desks quote the smile as vanna and volga positions (H2, one sentence; vanna–volga pricing is out of scope); Heston's correlation is vanna made into a model knob (D3). Last line, no formula; pull to D3 or back to D1 ("the cents left over after Raj's clock and bend are these").

## Why this shape
- Three rounds before any name, because the two Greeks that matter are stories (a changed mind, a quiet weekend) before they are derivatives; the sign flip is learned by getting round 2 wrong.
- One static poster instead of a 3-D probe: the research note's own warning (3-D Greeks are the weakest-explained thing on the web) and the brief ("do not make it look like rocket science"). The planes carry printed values and Natenberg's curves so they do not read as weather.
- Hull's Table 14.5 is replaced by the same grid on Raj's real position, because the reader already owns that book from C4/D1.
- Lightness: three figures; the second row of Greeks is captioned, footnoted with Wilmott, and given explicit permission to be forgotten.
